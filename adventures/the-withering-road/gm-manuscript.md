# VEILBOUND ADVENTURE 01 — THE WITHERING ROAD
## Adventure overview and GM-only truth · Edition 2.1 approved narrative draft

**GM SPOILERS.** Five intended sessions; about four starting Level 1 characters; advancement by actual GM-declared Milestones rather than session count. Chapters 2–4 are independent and may be played in **any order**. This is new adventure material, not a Veilbound rules amendment or a claim of encounter certification. The complete story, clue paths, handouts and decisions are authored; new spider combat profiles remain deliberately provisional.

> **Premise:** Dunmere's medicinal grayleaf is rotting, the public books do not explain the shortage, and an intact emergency reserve sits at distant Ember Watch. A frightened quartermaster concealed a mundane failure. The party can restore trust, secure relief and decide what accountability costs—if it can get the medicine home.

### Read this first: the truth

Three weeks before the opening, a grayleaf wagon convoy met an impassable section of the established road during sustained rain. **Renn Calder**, Dunmere's quartermaster, authorized a diversion to **Old Kiln Rest**, an abandoned relay with insufficient roofing. The wet layover damaged several bundles. Its cargo was split: crates **17-01, 17-02, 17-04 and 17-06** eventually reached Dunmere's municipal depot; **17-03 and 17-05** were left at the relay pending recovery and are still unusable or require inspection. Rain damaged goods in returned crates too. No one magically corrupted the grayleaf. There is no organized theft behind Lot 17.

Renn knew the diversion had caused avoidable loss and allowed the public notice to state that the lot was **“held for inspection,”** rather than admitting the scale of spoilage and the stranded crates. His motive is fear of blame and panic, not personal enrichment. This does not absolve him. **Edda Marr**, the honest record keeper, retained entries and amendments; **Hale**, the road soldier, knows the routes and enforces containment but does not know of the deceit; **Mira Fen**, the ordinary healer, needs uncontaminated supplies urgently.

**Ember Watch** has an independently accounted-for **dry grayleaf reserve**. Its steward, **Naeris**, is a Low Priest of Fire who considers accountable distribution a sacred duty. She is not required to withhold relief from a credible emergency: sound testimony, a practical distribution plan, inspection, or limited supervised release can all be persuasive. **Talla Fen**, Mira's courier daughter, took a message there after the shortage became serious. Bad weather and an injured ankle disrupted her return; she remains **alive, resting and able to speak**. She carries Mira's family ring, which has a detectable but inactive Life / Restoration Veil signature: a personal identifier, **not** a medical cure or a spell source. Her message and testimony help, but are not indispensable.

The abandoned shelter adjoins a limestone cave whose outer areas contain a **small colony of mundane cave spiders**. Deep within, an unusually aggressive **Veil-touched Dire Giant Spider** guards the Heartweb Chamber. The spider is a natural, territorial animal whose inward Veil alteration supports Vigor and regeneration—not an agent of Renn, a caster, or the cause of the spoiled medicine. The nearby **Hunter of the Woods** is a different, formidable territorial predator; its trail and habitat should be respected. Both creatures can survive a completed adventure.

**Sessions 2–4 = any permutation.** The chapters label themselves by name rather than an immutable session number. Players can split chapters, visit Ember Watch early or return to an unsolved scene. Preserve actual world time and resources. Every branch contains enough independent evidence to petition Naeris; no single clue, fight, magic technique or puzzle is mandatory.

### Why investigate more than one branch?

**Three parallel needs, not three keys to a locked door.** Session 1 reveals that the village must (1) establish a credible account and recruit people to help, (2) verify which goods and shipping records are trustworthy, and (3) learn what happened at Old Kiln Rest and whether a relief shipment can travel safely. The **Market** chiefly solves the *people/legitimacy* problem; the **Depot** chiefly solves the *proof/equipment* problem; the **Cavern** chiefly solves the *route/physical evidence* problem. Each also offers an independent lead to Ember Watch. Following all three gives the fullest and best-supported relief effort; the players need **not** finish three branches to locate, visit or petition Ember Watch.

**Player-facing destination:** Ember Watch is known from Session 1 as the location of a possible separate dry reserve; Talla went there and Naeris handles custody. The first completed investigation typically **confirms** that reserve and provides something usable in making the request. Further branches offer corroboration, assistance and safer logistics, not permission to travel there. If the party goes there early, run the credible provisional-release response and let remaining branches matter as follow-up aid, accountability and transport preparation.

### Six orders of the independent middle chapters

Every sequence below is valid. Give the party **only** advantages actually earned through testimony, investigation, repair or rescue. The later chapters' core facts remain available if an earlier branch goes badly.

| Chosen order | Advantage on entering second branch | Advantage on entering third branch | Strongest potential finale benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market → Depot → Cavern** | Edda's attested account can support lawful supervised entry; Jorin's descriptions identify useful crate numbers. | Original depot records pinpoint crates 17-03/05 and the signed diversion, clarifying what to recover at Old Kiln Rest. | Authenticated proof, community volunteers and an assessed route. |
| **Market → Cavern → Depot** | Jorin's testimony makes the old relay and likely wagon approach easier to identify. | The recovered dispatch gives specific authorizations and numbers to compare against depot originals. | Independent eyewitness, field and record corroboration. |
| **Depot → Market → Cavern** | Signed order and manifest discrepancies let the party ask Renn and witnesses precise, credible questions. | Witness descriptions and matched cargo numbers direct attention to the correct refuge and road conditions. | A strong accountability case backed by workers and physical proof. |

Do not add arbitrary check bonuses, experience awards or automatic NPC loyalty.

### Adventure milestones — named GM award windows

For this restricted Level 1–3 adventure, use **two different group-story achievements**, declared and recorded by the GM. These are **expected windows, not automatic session-completion awards**; if players take a different route or an investigation stretches beyond one sitting, recognize the corresponding meaningful event where it genuinely occurs. Grant **one level per event**, never both levels for the same achievement.

| Story window | Qualifying group achievement | Intended progression |
|---|---|---|
| **After the first substantial investigation breakthrough** (usually end of **Session 2**) | The party establishes an actionable, verified fact about Lot 17 **and** acts on it to advance the village's relief effort: e.g., secures credible community cooperation at Market; preserves the reconcilable source inventory at Depot; or recovers the dispatch and useful road findings from Cavern. Neither entering the site nor winning a fight suffices on its own. | **Level 1 → Level 2**, usually before Session 3. |
| **After the relief expedition becomes actionable** (usually end of **Session 4**) | The party has established a credible way to obtain and transport safe grayleaf: a request Naeris can evaluate plus realistic custody, transport or distribution arrangements. This may be earned with fewer than all three branches, or after early watch contact. A specific optional boss or secret archive is not required. | **Level 2 → Level 3**, ideally before Session 5. |

**Expected table rhythm:** Session 1 L1 → first chosen middle chapter L1 → declared Milestone One → remaining middle chapters L2 → declared Milestone Two → finale L3. A party that reaches the cavern first should meet calibrated Level 1 **lesser-spider** opposition with escape options; the dire spider is a visibly dangerous **optional** encounter and does **not** scale to party level. Players may leave the Heartweb Chamber and return later. Avoid promising a rules-complete combat until spider profiles are authored and tested.

**Leveling does not grant a free rest.** Carry forward HP, Vigor, VP, wounds, equipment, ammunition and elapsed-time recovery deadlines according to Veilbound's actual progression rules; add only legal level gains. The GM records which distinct story event was recognized, when, and each legal character-sheet change. No XP threshold, Anointed personal Mission beat or session number is a substitute for the GM's declaration.

### Village at a glance

- **Dunmere:** modest rain-worn rural market; bakery and communal hearth in the square, public ledger, healer's room, quartermaster's office, municipal depot on the edge.
- **Old Kiln Rest:** abandoned roofless wagon relay along the eastern old road, with a limestone refuge/cavern close by.
- **Ember Watch:** guarded storehouse and small Fire-aligned place of devotion further along the maintained watch route; the dry reserve is catalogued separately from Lot 17.
- **The fork:** signs and public road sketch distinguish the relay/cavern route, the ordinary depot road and Ember Watch. No hidden location gate.

### Three questions, many answers

| Question | Market evidence | Depot evidence | Cavern evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Where did Lot 17 go?** | Wagon hand recounts Old Kiln Rest diversion | Original-versus-receipt manifest exposes crates 03/05 absent | Wagon marks and rain refuge show missing cargo |
| **Who authorized it?** | Edda's ledger “R.C.” and Renn's statements | Signed duplicate diversion order | Sealed dispatch names quartermaster authorization |
| **Is there safe relief elsewhere?** | Separate reserve entry at Ember Watch | Reserve voucher in ordinary vault; protected duplicate in optional archive | Reserve reference in case; courier note supports it |

**Clue guarantee:** Examining an available core source supplies its literal facts; checks may reveal context, speed or corroboration but cannot make all three routes vanish. The original depot vault—not the hidden room—has a reserve record. The abandoned refuge—not the Heartweb boss—has the dispatch. Edda's records—not Renn's voluntary confession—supply the market route. Destroyed documents stay destroyed; alternate people and locations persist.

### Truth versus rumor

| Rumor | Actual position |
|---|---|
| “Someone stole all the grayleaf.” | A split and spoiled shipment created a genuine shortage; theft is unsupported |
| “Naeris refuses all requests because Fire demands it.” | Naeris is protecting an accounted reserve; justified limited or full release is possible |
| “The spider or Hunter caused the blight.” | Wildlife threatens travel; neither spoiled the herb |
| “A gifted healer or ring can mend the entire lot.” | The ring is inactive; ordinary medicine is still needed; actual PC techniques only do what their rules allow |

### The player-choice feedback loop

Record **witnesses**, **evidence quality**, **NPC commitments**, **depot entry/damage**, **road and predator status**, **equipment recovered**, **actual elapsed hours**, **party resource state**, and **whether villagers have been told a truthful account**. These notes—not an artificial reputation meter—determine the finale. Examples:

- If Renn publicly exposed, he may stop volunteering help while villagers cooperate with Edda.
- If Renn privately accepts restitution, he may provide wagons and signatures but Edda may insist on an independent public correction later.
- If the depot was forcibly entered, Hale can challenge whether papers were tampered with; a surviving clerk or original counterpart can authenticate them.
- If the cavern was mapped safely, players can propose a reliable transport route; the Hunter may still be alive and territorial.
- If the party avoided the deep spider, the Heartweb Chamber remains occupied. This is not an unresolved main-quest failure.

### Magic, mechanics and difficulty contract

Use **Core Resolution**: relevant `d20 + Ability + narrow applicable Training + modifiers` versus `10 + Difficulty Modifier` if uncertain and consequential. Players may deduce visible clues without a check. Ordinary social interactions depend on the approach and NPC motives; there is no universal Persuasion/Investigation/Stealth proficiency. Canonical Take 10/Take 20 applies only when genuinely safe time permits. No generic assisted-roll, alert-clock or skill-challenge subsystem is implied.

**Primary combat-production limit:** The **Hunter (B06-EN-03)** and **Ordinary Human Soldier (B06-EN-02)** have source-backed but **uncalibrated** fixtures; the **mundane cave spiders and Veil-touched Dire Giant Spider** are not yet complete executable stat blocks. This module specifies scenes and tactical intentions, not invented finalized spider attacks. For a rules-complete combat, source-authored spiders need final timing, movement, attack, web and toxin implementation and verification. Do **not** present boss severity as tested or auto-award it an XP/CR value. A party can still complete the mystery without fighting new creatures. See the Creature Status appendix.

Session 1 uses P-1 (public notice) and P-2 (public route sketch), the Market M-1–M-3, the Depot D-1–D-5, the Cavern C-1–C-4, and Ember Watch F-1–F-3. Draw the **seven depot zones** and **seven cavern points** from their chapter schematics; they are route diagrams, not measured tactical maps. Know where the essential clues remain if the players avoid combat, refuse entry or visit the chapters out of order. Prepare Talla and Naeris for early visits. Before beginning, establish how the group's characters know or meet each other; avoid forcing a single PC motive.

# VEILBOUND ADVENTURE 01 — THE WITHERING ROAD
## Session 1: A Village in Need

**GM manuscript · Chapter draft v1 · 1 November 2026**  
**Intended table:** approximately four Level 1 characters · **Target:** 3–4 hours  
**Scope:** Session 1 only. Session 2–4 branches are *The Market's Stories*, *The Sealed Depot* and *The Old Road Cavern*, in any player-chosen order. Session 5 is *Ember Watch and the Reckoning*.  
**Authority:** Adventure-local fiction, not a canonical rule or world-lore change. The current Veilbound rules supersede any example in this chapter.

> **Creative direction:** Start with ordinary people, their voluntary traditions and the cost of a broken promise. In Dunmere, visible Veil workings are uncommon and precious. Faith is lived in shared commitments; a public rite does not mechanically cast a spell. Let the players experience the village before asking them to save it.

## Running this chapter at a glance

### What must be true at the end

Every PC has had an introduction or meaningful opportunity to act; the party understands something of everyday Pillar culture; the people of Dunmere feel like persons rather than quest dispensers; the spoiled Lot 17 and its human consequences are clear; **all three investigation leads are independently available**; the players decide what to investigate next. No conclusion about guilt or magic is required.

| Beat | Suggested time | GM action | Player-facing decision |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1. Rain on the market | 15–20 min | Establish Dunmere, ordinary trade, shared customs | Where are you, and what catches your attention? |
| 2. People before heroes | 25–35 min | Invite short character-introduction scenes | Who do you engage with, help or avoid? |
| 3. The Hearth and the Ledger | 20–25 min | Show *lived* Fire, Kinship, Life and Order traditions | What do your characters think of these practices? |
| 4. Lot 17 is opened | 25–35 min | Play a visible contradiction, not a lore speech | Protect, examine, question, calm or walk away? |
| 5. Four accounts | 40–55 min | Make key NPCs available without scripting interrogation order | Whom do you trust; which facts can you verify? |
| 6. A request, not a command | 15–25 min | Give a concrete invitation to help and room for refusal | Under what terms will you become involved? |
| 7. Three roads | 15–20 min | Reveal three independently actionable leads | Which lead do you pursue first, and why? |

The table can linger in any beat. Do not pad an early resolution merely to fill the clock; if players initiate a branch during Session 1, honor the action and end at a sensible break.

### The GM knows; the players do not

Three weeks ago a grayleaf convoy was diverted to the roofless **Old Kiln Rest** relay after the original road became impassable. Heavy rain spoiled part of **Lot 17**. **Renn Calder** authorized the detour and concealed the extent of the damage, afraid of public panic and losing his post. This is **ordinary spoilage, not Veil corruption, a curse, or evidence of a magical thief**. A clean dry reserve remains at **Ember Watch**, under **Naeris**, a Low Priest of Fire charged with accountable distribution. A missing courier, **Talla Fen**, is alive, exhausted and sheltered at Ember Watch in the default version. The territorial **Hunter of the Woods** is an animal, not an accomplice.

Do not telegraph this solution as the right answer during Session 1. A villager's theft allegation is a believable *rumor*, and every NPC has a limited perspective.

### The magical boundary

- **Mira Fen is an experienced ordinary healer**, not a free source of Restoration spells. Her skill, ethics and physical care matter even when there is no caster present.
- Mira once carried a **ring bearing an observable Life / Restoration Veil signature**, now carried by courier **Talla at Ember Watch**. The ring is **not** in the depot, old-road cache, or abandoned-refuge dispatch case. A note recovered there may mention the ring, but **Talla retains the actual heirloom** unless players change its custody through play. It is an identification heirloom, **not a cure, magical antidote, or secretly active item**. Its significance can be learned by mundane testimony without magical Inspection.
- A player character may use an **actually known, legal** technique if they choose; pay normal AP/VP and obey access, targets and effect boundaries. Universal Veil Inspection is relevant only if the actual item is **held** by the inspecting character and the canonical cost is met; it does not automatically disclose history or hidden function.
- The GM should neither stage a gratuitous magical display nor punish a character for using their own rare ability. An actual working is an event villagers may notice and interpret differently; it does not make its effect larger than the rules allow.
- There are **no universal bonuses, healing effects, prophetic messages, regional Pillar modifiers or mandatory Faith checks** attached to Dunmere's traditions.

## Beat 1 — Rain on the Market

**GM purpose:** Communicate a grounded world worth caring about before introducing a crisis. Let the players choose how their characters enter it.

**Read aloud:**

> The rain has passed, but Dunmere has not dried. Water drips from patched awnings onto the market stones. A potter covers her wares with a blanket. A baker sets today's first warm loaf beside a little hearth rather than on the sale board. The smith at the corner shop argues good-naturedly with a farmer over the repair of a wagon pin.
>
> Beneath the public notice board, a rain-darkened ledger hangs behind glass. People stop there to read the day's deliveries, as they might read the weather. Most carry on with their errands. For now, it is an ordinary morning.
>
> Where are you in the square, and what are you doing?

Give a few seconds to each player. If someone needs a prompt: arriving with a wagon, seeking work, visiting an acquaintance, attending to personal business, sheltering from rain, looking for provisions, or already living here are all possible. **Do not declare that the party is already a hired group.**

**Dunmere in three impressions:** The village is small enough for people to recognize repeated mistakes; it is neither uniformly pious nor hostile to other devotions; and a repair, an honest account, or a shared meal matters more to its residents than abstract pronouncements about power.

**If players explore freely:** The baker, notice board, smith, public hearth and healer's threshold can all host an interaction. Do not rush to the shipment until characters have had some opportunity to be people.

## Beat 2 — People Before Heroes

Choose **one short invitation per PC**, not a compulsory test. Use the players' own concepts and avoid implying a particular Archetype must do a particular thing.

| Situation | What the GM presents | Plausible choices and consequences |
|---|---|---|
| A wet load | A carter needs a second pair of hands to move a crate away from water | Help, advise, improvise, decline; cooperation earns gratitude and gossip, no automatic reward roll |
| An unfinished promise | A neighbor has saved a parcel for someone who has not arrived | Deliver it, inquire, leave it; may establish ordinary local relationships |
| A difficult purchase | A vendor doubts whether a traveler can pay | Negotiate, offer work, walk away; do not turn every conversation into a Presence check |
| A private concern | An elderly villager asks whether the healer is taking new patients | Answer, accompany them or admit not knowing; introduces Mira naturally |

**GM question:** “What would people here notice first about you?” Follow with “Is this someone you've known before, or a first meeting?” Never require a prepared backstory monologue or invented shared history.

Characters who begin as strangers can come together when they all witness the shipment incident. Characters already acquainted can play their relationships rather than reintroducing themselves to one another.

## Beat 3 — The Hearth and the Ledger

**GM purpose:** Reveal how Pillars occupy daily life through free choices, repeated customs and disagreements. The practices are **adventure-local customs**, not new universal religions, miracles, mechanical rites or proof of literal divine speech.

### A small market custom

Near the communal hearth, the baker sets aside the first sound loaf of the day. On ordinary mornings, someone carries it to a household in need before the remaining loaves are sold. The hearth is kept dry after storms, and anyone tending it may recall **Fire** as warmth and responsibility, **Kinship** as mutual duty, and **Life** as care.

**Read aloud, if the players stop:**

> “First warmth belongs to someone who cannot afford it,” the baker says, wrapping the loaf. “That's what my mother said, anyway. Some call it a promise to Fire, some to Kinship. I think cold people are cold no matter what name you use.”

Allow different NPCs to interpret the same act differently: one believes a small prayer matters; another says the actual giving is what counts. A village resident might faithfully hold both views. **Neither person should declare an authoritative cosmological explanation to the party.**

### The public ledger

Edda's village ledger is kept in sight of the community as an act of **Order**: goods received, promises made, obligations recorded. Citizens disagree about whether Order means following procedure or recording the truth when procedure has failed. A resident may object that a private family matter should not be publicly exposed; the argument raises **Kinship** and **Liberty** alongside Order.

**GM-only metaphysical alignment:** In Veilbound, Pillars arise from and are reinforced by recurring meaningful volition and lived patterns. Words, symbols, prayer and ritual help sustain shared meaning; they are not a separate magical fuel, nor does an empty gesture automatically reinforce a Pillar. Dunmere's customs should therefore include real, voluntary choices with small costs: give away the loaf, tell an uncomfortable truth, or protect a neighbor despite inconvenience.

**If a player asks if a Pillar is a god:** Answer in-world through the chosen speaker. “My grandmother prayed to Fire as if it listened,” or “I don't know what listens; the hearth still needs tending” are valid *views*. The GM need not reveal the setting's complete metaphysical foundation in Session 1.

**If characters participate:** Thank them in fiction. No +1, temporary VP, supernatural blessing, free recovery or new Pact follows.

## Beat 4 — Lot 17 Is Opened

**Trigger:** Once the players have had an opening interaction, a supply cart arrives in the square. Its side is marked **LOT 17 — GRAYLEAF**. The cart has only part of the consignment, visibly degraded. A familiar public notice behind Edda's glass reads: “Shipment held for inspection; distribution pending.”

**Read aloud:**

> The carter pulls back the canvas and stops. Beneath the wrapping, the herb bundles are dark at the seams. The sharp, unpleasant smell reaches the closest onlookers first.
>
> Mira Fen pushes through the gathering crowd. She takes one leaf, rubs it carefully between finger and thumb, and looks back at the notice board. “This lot cannot go to my patients,” she says. “Not like this.”
>
> A voice from the crowd answers, “Then where did the rest of it go?” Another says the word *stolen*. Someone calls for Renn. People begin speaking over one another.

Do not roll to notice wet, damaged goods. A character examining the batch can see water stains, split bindings and bad odor. Identifying the *degree and ordinary cause* of spoilage may warrant an **Intellect + actually learned Medicine** check if uncertain and consequential, using the source rules and circumstances; the prior authored sample used target 12 and 240 seconds, **not a universal fixed DC**. Failure does not erase obvious damage or force the conclusion of magic.

### Immediate playable decisions

- **Protect people:** Organize the crowd, ask Mira whether anyone has used this batch, help keep unsafe herbs away from patients or collect clean water and supplies.
- **Investigate:** Examine packaging, read the notice, ask when the cart arrived or which bundles are missing. The party may learn that there is **a discrepancy**, not yet its full cause.
- **Challenge someone:** Question the carter, request Renn's explanation, or ask Edda to show the original record. The involved NPCs respond according to what they know, not a universal social success table.
- **Use magic:** A legal PC technique may affect an eligible subject exactly as written. **No known technique automatically restores an entire spoiled shipment, identifies a liar, or cures a village.**
- **Leave:** The characters may choose not to intervene. The incident is not a magical compulsion or forced recruitment. Let them pursue their own business and preserve later opportunities for involvement.

### Stakes that belong to people

Mira has patients who need safe supplies, but they are not all dying on a countdown invented to force compliance. Families worry that the shortage will grow. Renn's fear of panic has already cost the village trust. No medically unsupported disease, toxin packet or mass cure is introduced here.

## Beat 5 — Four Accounts

Keep the principals available in the public square, healer's room, storehouse office and record room. Use **player-chosen approach**, not an NPC dialogue tree. An NPC may be busy or reluctant; never make one particular check the sole method to learn the next location.

### Mira Fen — healer and moral center

**First impression:** Practical clothes, stained hands, quietly exhausted. She cares about the patients more than who wins an argument. **Wants:** safe, verified care and relief supply. **Knows:** the arriving goods are suspect; there should be more usable stock than this; her courier daughter Talla has not returned from the eastern road. **Does not know:** Renn's full cover-up or where every crate went. **Will not:** pronounce the ruined herbs safe or claim a miracle she cannot perform.

**Sample lines:** “I can treat what I can reach. I cannot hand people rotten medicine and call it hope.” / “If there is a clean reserve, find who can release it.”

**Veil connection, kept precious:** Mira once owned the Life/Restoration-marked courier ring now carried by Talla. It is meaningful as a family token and identity mark, **not an activated healing artifact**. If asked, Mira can explain that Veil workings exist, that healers should never promise them as routine replacement for sound judgment, and that she is not equipped to undo this entire shortage by magic. She does not have to identify who might cast a particular technique.

### Edda Marr — record keeper

**First impression:** Precise handwriting, patient but formidable attention to discrepancies. **Wants:** an accurate public account. **Knows:** the displayed note is a summary; the original ledger lists separate consignments and a changed route. **Does not know:** precisely who concealed which physical crate. **Will not:** quietly forge or destroy a record.

**Sample lines:** “A line can be correct and still conceal a great deal.” / “Ask what the entry says. Then ask what it does *not* say.”

Edda can direct anyone to the **market witnesses and public records**, one of three independent leads. A request for a closer look now can reveal an inconsistency but does not require running the entire Social chapter in Session 1.

### Renn Calder — quartermaster

**First impression:** Composed presentation, strained attention to what others overhear. **Wants:** prevent panic, preserve his standing and buy time. **Knows:** he authorized the diversion, some goods spoiled, and his public statement withheld crucial detail. **Will not:** confess the full story unprompted merely because a PC asks a direct question.

**Sample lines:** “The notice says held for inspection because that's what was ordered.” / “We cannot have every wet crate made into an accusation.”

Play him as **responsible but human**, not the obvious villain in disguise. If confronted with a sound, specific inconsistency, he may concede a partial fact. A failed social approach need not shut down access to the records, depot or cavern.

### Hale — road soldier

**First impression:** Rain-specked shield, deliberate concern about people crowding tainted goods. **Wants:** keep unverified medicines out of circulation and enforce legitimate restrictions. **Knows:** routes to the **municipal depot** and **old road**; a caravan passed through after a closure. **Does not know:** Renn intentionally concealed the extent of spoilage. **Will not:** attack strangers simply for asking questions.

**Sample lines:** “The road is open. What I won't do is let a bad crate pass without a name on the paper.” / “If you want facts, start where the crates were counted.”

Hale can point out the **Sealed Depot** and explain that the **Old Road Cavern** sits beside the disused wagon refuge. Distinguish legitimate custody from blanket hostility. Ordinary arguments use canonical noncombat procedures when a roll is appropriate.

### Someone the PCs may hear about, not a required cameo

**Talla Fen**, Mira's courier daughter, went toward Ember Watch. **Naeris**, the Fire-aligned priest who oversees the reserve, has responsibility for its safe and accountable release. The party may learn both names here, but should not need to meet either person in Session 1. Do not say Naeris speaks literally for Fire or that Fire commanded a particular release decision.

## Beat 6 — A Request, Not a Command

After people have reacted, Mira asks whether the characters can help establish what happened and secure safe medicine. Edda offers to preserve a copy of the day's publicly accessible notice. Hale can provide directions; Renn may offer a limited official task if players seek wages or authority.

**Read aloud (adapt to how events unfolded):**

> Mira looks from the spoiled leaves to the people still waiting nearby. “I don't need someone to declare a villain. I need someone who can find out what happened—and help us get a supply we can trust.”
>
> “Someone ought to make a record of it,” Edda adds. “One that cannot simply be corrected after the fact.”
>
> Nobody tells you where you must go. For a moment, the square waits to see what you decide.

### Invitations by motive, not by Archetype

- **Care and community:** Help people who may go without basic treatment.
- **Truth and duty:** Discover whether officials misled the village and what should be done.
- **Work and reputation:** Seek payment, patronage or recognition through a legitimate service.
- **Curiosity and conviction:** Pursue unusual evidence, test a belief or act on a personal obligation.

Ask players: “If you choose to help, what would make this worth doing for your character?” Accept their answer. No PC is required to have the same motive. If the party refuses the initial request, do not confiscate control; Edda's record, Hale's road warning and the market rumor remain available for later re-engagement. A refusal has ordinary fictional consequences, not a punishment disguised as a rule.

### The three reasons to continue — make each meaningful

Before the party chooses where to go, the villagers should show that the shortage has **three different practical dimensions**:

- **People and trust (Market):** Edda needs witnesses who will stand behind a truthful account, and Mira needs people willing to help when safe medicine arrives.
- **Proof and equipment (Depot):** the physical crates and receipts need reconciling; obtaining reliable records, coverings or cargo support could strengthen an appeal for emergency supplies.
- **What happened and how to travel (Old Road Cavern):** Old Kiln Rest holds independent evidence, while washed-out passages and territorial wildlife could make a return freight journey dangerous.

When a player asks, “Why not just go to Ember Watch?”, tell them the ordinary road to the Watch is open to travel. A credible immediate plea might win **limited relief**, but a durable response also needs honest records, trusted helpers and transport. These are **incentives to investigate**, not mandatory checkboxes. The GM can remind players of the still-unsolved needs through natural NPC dialogue after each subsequent branch.

## Beat 7 — Three Roads Forward

Present **all three** choices clearly and at the same time; no chapter is gated behind another. The players choose order freely for Sessions 2–4.

| Lead | What anyone can know in Session 1 | Where to begin | Expected primary experience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Market's Stories** | Vendors and wagon hands disagree about the shipment's arrival and departure; Edda has public entries | Market stalls and records room in Dunmere | **Social:** witnesses, trust, truth versus comfort |
| **The Sealed Depot** | Some crates and duplicate paperwork remain under controlled storage; Hale knows the route and authority responsible | Municipal depot on the village edge | **Skills & puzzles:** entry decisions, physical evidence, reconstruction |
| **The Old Road Cavern** | The diverted wagon used Old Kiln Rest; tracks continue near a limestone cavern; animals have been disturbed there | Public old-road sketch and east trail | **Combat & danger:** warned approach, tactical risk, avoidance or retreat |

**Read aloud:**

> By late afternoon, three places keep returning to the conversation: the market, where people remember different versions of the same day; the depot, where the crates and paperwork were left; and the old road, where the wagons were driven after the rain.
>
> Each may hold a different part of the truth. Which do you investigate first?

If the party asks “Why not go straight to Ember Watch?”, say what the characters know: it holds or is reported to hold a separately accounted-for dry reserve, and Naeris has an obligation to verify releases. **Let them go.** The final chapter is not an invisible level-locked location; early petition may yield partial relief or new information. Reorganize scenes around their actual decision.

**Session close prompt:** “What is your first move, who should know your plan, and what are you hoping to prove?” Record the choice and any NPC promises or expectations.

## GM reference — continuity and responses

### Evidence that is already available without special powers

- Lot 17 was at least partly wet and is unsafe to distribute without evaluation.
- The notice says it is being held for inspection; this does not explain the missing portion.
- A route change, a depot and an old road are ordinary matters of public knowledge.
- The existence of a separate reserve is credible but not a promise of automatic release.

**Withhold until earned or volunteered:** the complete cover-up, the exact fate of the missing cargo, the full Old Kiln Rest history, and evidence that Renn knowingly concealed specific spoilage.

### When and why to roll

Use current **Core Resolution** and **Non-Combat Play**, not D&D skill lists. The player states an approach. The GM determines whether the result is uncertain and consequential, then uses a relevant Ability, **actually learned narrow Training** and honest situational modifiers. Everyday observation or background familiarity often needs no roll. Social outcomes depend on the NPC's interests and the approach, not a universal Persuasion bonus. Do not invent automatic group-help, extended influence clocks or contested stealth rules.

**Examples, not additional rules:** an ordinary diagnostic check may clarify wet-herb spoilage; Awareness may notice a wet seal; Intellect with a genuinely applicable regional trade Training may identify an entry convention; a Presence-based appeal may persuade a worried bystander to stop repeating a rumor. None is mandatory, and no success produces omniscient knowledge.

### No mandatory magic

A village custom is **not** a spell. A ring bearing a Veil signature is **not** a healing kit. A player-owned legal magical ability remains fully available if chosen, but the shipment's contamination is mundane. A spectacular new Veil occurrence is unnecessary to establish a fantasy setting. The distinctive element here is that **human practice, belief and deliberate choices interact with a real metaphysical world**, while magic is rare enough in this village to matter when invoked.

### Common improvisations

| Player action | GM handling |
|---|---|
| “We publicly accuse Renn now.” | Let him rebut, let Edda ask for evidence, track trust; do not force a confession or shut down the mystery |
| “We demand the full ledger.” | Establish lawful public access and Edda's interests; provide relevant visible entries, with deeper reconciliation in the market chapter |
| “We take or burn the herbs.” | Describe immediate implications for evidence and care; no magical toxin damage or forced punishment |
| “We treat the patients.” | Allow source-legal ordinary care and any actually known techniques; no free mass cure or invented HP restoration |
| “We offer to pay for relief ourselves.” | Treat this as a real proposal; Ember Watch still needs proper handling and release, but personal funding may change negotiations |
| “We travel to a branch right now.” | Allow it and preserve actual chronology; end the session at a natural scene boundary rather than pretending they cannot leave |
| “We refuse.” | Respect the refusal and allow separate reasons to re-engage; do not apply a railroaded threat or divine punishment |

# THE MARKET'S STORIES
## Independent middle chapter · Social investigation · GM version 1

**Run at any point after the opening, even if the party has never seen the depot or cavern.** Target one substantial session, about 3–4 hours. Do not turn the scenes into mandatory interviews in a fixed order. This chapter is about obtaining **credible human testimony and a shared account** of Lot 17, not just finding three information tokens.

### At a glance

**Immediate hook:** Edda invites inspection of her public amendment ledger; merchants are arguing about “missing” grayleaf and a wagon-hand is ready to leave for seasonal work. **Primary objective:** establish the divergence between the official notice and what witnesses saw, then decide whose account Dunmere is prepared to trust. **Guaranteed independent facts:** diversion to Old Kiln Rest; authorization marked R.C.; separately filed Ember Watch reserve. **Potential rewards:** certified ledger extract; a named witness; merchant transport cooperation; public correction; Renn's willingness to make restitution. **Costs:** loss of trust, public panic, witness fear, strained relationships if the party handles the dispute harshly.

### Opening scene — Rain, rumor, and a price

> The awnings have been mended since the spoiled crate was opened, but the argument beneath them has not. At the grain stall, two neighbors accuse each other of hoarding. A cart driver insists that a wagon headed east never came back. A merchant says the account books prove otherwise. Edda, across the square, is copying a correction onto fresh paper. She has not hung it up yet.
>
> “Someone ought to ask the people who unloaded those wagons,” she says. “Before everyone decides they already know.”

Ask the players whom they speak with, what they want to learn and whether they promise discretion. The market is active; they can consult stalls, read the public board or head to Renn directly. Common knowledge includes the depot location, Old Kiln Rest road, and Ember Watch's existence; no clue requires previous chapters.

### Scene S1 — Three accounts at the stalls

Present **three independent witnesses with different interests**. None is secretly a thief.

| Person | What they truly know | Why they hesitate | What changes cooperation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jorin Vale**, wagon-hand | Convoy diverted toward roofless Old Kiln Rest; two numbered crates left there after rain | Afraid an admission will cost hauling work | Listen without declaring him criminal; offer to put his exact words on record; help him secure future lawful work |
| **Sella Brook**, dry-goods trader | Saw four marked crates arrive at depot; inspection labels were fresh while bindings beneath were wet | Angry that losses may be passed to merchants | Treat her economic loss seriously; seek independent verification instead of accepting an accusation |
| **Perrin Holt**, cartwright | Replaced a damaged wheel pin after a washed-out road made passage dangerous | Resents being blamed for delay | Ask about road conditions, offer fair payment for work, distinguish broken road from sabotage |

Give the factual testimony when characters genuinely earn access to the conversation or investigate the relevant public event. A roll is not required to receive ordinary facts from a willing speaker. Presence may help when convincing a frightened witness to give a **signed statement**; Awareness may notice someone avoiding questions; Intellect with a narrow relevant trade discipline may reconstruct the chronology. No universal Persuasion or Interrogation Training applies.

**Optional friction:** Sella demands to know whether the party will accuse all carters. Jorin says he will not sign anything that simply calls him a thief. Perrin will show the pin and repair log if asked politely. A clumsy confrontation does not destroy their knowledge: the party can still consult Edda's record or depot receipts.

**Discoverable detail:** Four crates reached Dunmere, not six. Jorin names Old Kiln Rest even if he refuses to sign. The practical shortcut to the cavern remains public regardless.

### Scene S2 — Edda's ledger

Edda's record room contains the **public amendment**, a surviving **earlier entry**, and the **separate-reserve line**. She allows ordinary public reading and makes certified copies for a concrete purpose; she may refuse handing away unique originals without safeguards.

**The GM presents these entries visibly:**

- *Lot 17: six numbered crates dispatched; rain diversion to Old Kiln Rest; R.C. approval recorded.*
- *Return inventory: four numbered crates received; contents under inspection.*
- *Ember Watch: dry emergency reserve held under separate custody; not part of the returned Lot 17.*
- *Public notice: Lot 17 held for inspection.*

The contradiction is between the notice's implied completeness and the actual split, not an automatically forged entire ledger. Edda originally recorded what staff delivered and later discovered that the notice failed to describe the loss. She needs the party's help finding someone willing to stand by the correction.

**Read aloud:**

> Edda turns the pages so everyone can see. One entry has a small notation beside it: *R.C.* The receiving count says four. The outgoing count says six. Neither is crossed out.
>
> “A record can be accurate,” she says, “and still be used to tell an untrue story.”

**Evidence reward:** If the party requests a copy, Edda can produce a dated, attested extract **without requiring a check**. Obtaining custody of the originals requires a stronger reason and may never be necessary. A thoughtful request can also persuade her to accompany a hearing or send a message to Naeris. Give **M-1** from [player-handouts.md](player-handouts.md); fill its attestation only when Edda actually signs it. **M-2** is an optional witness statement template (not a pre-signed admission), and **M-3** is a blank petition.

### Scene S3 — Renn's explanation

Renn's office is open during work hours and accessible by appointment or public petition; breaking in is not required. He does not know exactly which crates are still physically salvageable and cannot guarantee there is sufficient reserve for everyone. He **does know** he authorized the diversion and withheld the loss in the public notice.

**Renn's likely progression, not a scripted confession:**

1. **Without specific evidence:** “Held for inspection was the safest description we had.” He offers to check the ledger but refuses a blanket theft accusation.
2. **Presented with six outbound/four inbound:** He admits a diversion and claims the details were still being reconciled.
3. **Presented with witness or original authorization:** He concedes that he knew crates 03 and 05 were left at the relay and that wet bundles returned.
4. **Asked about the false impression:** He may admit fear of panic, lost trust and dismissal, especially if the party offers a feasible correction and return plan. Hostile threats may harden him or produce denial; he remains answerable through the paper trail.

**Read aloud (when he faces an authenticated contradiction):**

> Renn's hand stops on the edge of the desk. “I signed the diversion. The other road was gone. I did not order the rain to fall.”
>
> Edda's copy lies between you. He looks at the difference between four and six and does not touch it. “But I let them think the lot was merely waiting. That much was my choice.”

Ask what the players **want Renn to do now**: amend a public notice, sign a statement, provide a wagon, accompany them to Naeris, surrender responsibility to Edda, or simply stop obstructing. Choose his reaction from circumstances. If he refuses, Edda's records and other witnesses continue to matter.

### Scene S4 — The public dispute

A handful of villagers assemble near the ledger board. Sella worries that unsafe products will be quietly sold; Jorin fears he is being named a thief; Mira fears a prolonged hearing while patients wait; Edda insists on a durable account. Let players choose where and to whom they speak. A good argument need only be described by the player, not performed as an eloquent speech.

**Three broad, combinable approaches:**

| Choice | Immediate result | Later cost or benefit |
|---|---|---|
| **Open accounting** | Edda publishes documented counts and diversion; Jorin and Sella can speak | More public trust; Renn becomes guarded; some villagers demand discipline before relief |
| **Private restitution first** | Renn may quietly arrange transport, signature and correction deadline | Faster cooperation; Edda may distrust an unannounced promise; delayed truth must still be addressed |
| **Relief before blame** | Mira recruits helpers and vendors offer supplies while accusations are postponed | Immediate care benefits; misinformation may return at the finale if no record is made |

**Other player-led outcomes:** Defending Renn while insisting on truthful records; disputing testimony; making restitution conditional; publicly accusing Hale; negotiating repayment. Resolve by who believes whom and why. Do not declare universal successes or automatic town-wide attitude changes from one roll.

### Scene S5 — A practical reward, not a gold chest

A constructive outcome offers one or more **concrete commitments**. A vendor can promise covered canvas and carts; Jorin can guide the relay road; Edda can seal a verified extract; Renn can provide a signed petition or transport labor. These people are not obligated to provide free property without circumstances justifying it. The chapter's reward is what the party can actually use at Ember Watch.

**Suggested closing beat:**

> The market resumes its own argument over prices, but this time one page on Edda's board has been properly corrected. Or perhaps it has not, and the people around you know why. Either way, the roads still run east. Somewhere beyond them is medicine that has not been left in the rain.

**Milestone One candidate (if this is the first substantial branch achievement):** When the party secures actionable testimony/certification **and** meaningfully organizes the response, the GM may declare **The First Breakthrough** (L1→L2). Mere conversation, simply entering the market, or forcing a confession is not an automatic award. See the overview milestone procedure.

### Changing this chapter by visit order

- **First:** Jorin's testimony and Edda's records make the first coherent case. The other two adventure branches gain context but not access permission.
- **After the depot:** Show the receiving numbers to witnesses. Their testimony can authenticate the physical labels and reveal the human decision behind the duplicate order; no redundant compulsory arithmetic exercise.
- **After the cavern:** Renn faces the actual dispatch and wet goods. His explanations shorten; the emotional dilemma over public disclosure becomes larger, not smaller.
- **If the party already petitioned Naeris:** The market matters because somebody must distribute, record and transport relief; persuade people to stand by an honest request.

### If things go wrong

**Witness refuses:** He describes an ordinary fact off record, or Edda's entry remains. **Renn shuts the door:** Edda records an unanswered request; physical evidence still exists. **Crowd turns on a wagon-hand:** Hale may intervene to keep order; protect the innocent and make the trust consequence visible. **A player threatens someone:** Authorities respond proportionately, with negotiation and departure possible; do not turn a social chapter into guaranteed combat. **Party leaves without proof:** They still know the three leads from Session 1 and can return for a certified extract.

### Table-side checklist

- [ ] At least one merchant/wagon-hand has a personal stake distinct from the mystery.
- [ ] Party can read the three core ledger facts without a roll gate.
- [ ] Renn reacts according to actual evidence and the players' approach.
- [ ] Record whether the notice was corrected and whether Jorin/Sella/Edda/Renn promised assistance.
- [ ] The next site and Ember Watch remain reachable; no automatic milestone or rest.

**Core handouts:** M-1 Ledger extract, M-2 witness testimony slip (only if requested/certified), M-3 signed petition blank.

# THE SEALED DEPOT
## Independent middle chapter · Skills, puzzles and access choices · GM version 1

**Run in any player-chosen middle slot.** The municipal grayleaf depot is known from Session 1. No market witness or cavern dispatch is required. Target roughly 3–4 hours, with about four to six **substantial** obstacles actually encountered. This chapter preserves the approved Locked Design v1: multiple entries, individual consequences, two major puzzles and optional guards/social confrontation.

### At a glance

**Purpose:** Establish how Lot 17 was split, identify which crates were damaged and obtain credible records of the separately held Ember Watch reserve. **Essential evidence:** outbound crates `17-01` through `17-06`; inbound `17-01, 17-02, 17-04, 17-06`; diverted `17-03, 17-05`; signed route order `R.C.`; separate reserve voucher. **Optional discovery:** old protected archive behind the Three Obligations mechanism. **Guards:** Hale and a civilian watchkeeper; the worker and clerk do not automatically fight. **No single way in is privileged.**

### Approaching the depot

> The municipal depot stands on a low rise where runoff leaves a pale stain on the stone steps. Its receiving doors are iron-bound, but the working side is busy: a porter wrestles a covered crate off a trolley while a clerk holds an oilcloth over his papers. At the outer gate, Hale looks up from checking a wagon seal.
>
> “It's under inspection,” he says before anyone has asked. “That means keep bad medicine out, not keep honest people out. What exactly do you want to see?”

**A useful starting question:** “Are you requesting access, looking for a different way inside, or watching the place first?” Do not require the party to choose one of a set menu of solutions. If they act openly, Hale can arrange supervised inspection and clerk testimony given a credible purpose. If they attempt entry unobserved, use the physical architecture and actual timing; there is no universal Stealth contest.

### Site schematic (connections, not scale)

```text
                [A] Guarded yard and main gate
                           |
                [B] Receiving hall / manifest
                  /        |           \
       [C] Wet storage  [D] Clerk      [E] Service bay
                        office /           rear doors
                           |                 |
                        [F] Old vault   roof/upper gallery
                           |
               [G] Hidden archival room
                 (optional secret entry)
```

Players can reach B via the main door, E via workers or service shutter, and the upper gallery via a window or permitted stair access. The rooms physically connect through ordinary doors and aisles; no area exists solely behind an impossible single lock. F contains the **ordinary evidence**; G contains optional rewards.

## Area A — Guarded yard: access is an encounter

**Hale wants** unsafe grayleaf secured and credible visitors accounted for. **The watchkeeper wants** the gate and people safe. Both ask questions before using force where plausible. The schedule includes deliveries, worker changes and an ordinary posted opening period; planning, patience or a credible petition can matter.

| Method | Proposed adjudication under pressure | Advantage | Cost/failure fiction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Permission or official request | Roleplay; Presence only if needed, or relevant documentary/law knowledge | Lawful access, staff explanations, authenticated findings | Escort may restrict unattended searching; refusal is appealable |
| Offer useful work | Explain what labor is needed; genuine service, no required roll for routine tasks | Receiving areas, gratitude | Workers notice if visitors vanish into restricted rooms |
| Main padlock | **Precision + Lockwork vs 12** | Quiet reusable entry | Time; patrol approaches if genuinely nearby |
| Break padlock | **Strength vs 15** with suitable force/tools | No lock expertise | Sharp noise, visibly broken iron, liability |
| Pry door/hardware | **Strength vs 15** with leverage | Independent forceful entry | Obvious repair damage; might still fail if leverage impossible |
| Rear shutter | Assess timing; Lockwork **12** if secured; Agility for uncertain quiet movement | Avoid front entrance | Working staff may notice; forced shutters show marks |
| Upper window | **Strength + Climbing vs 12** for the accessible dry route; adjust wetness/height | Reaches gallery | Fall/noise risk according to actual support and legal Falling rules |
| Find/obtain a key | Speak to staff, search where a real key is kept or borrow lawfully | Undamaged access | Unauthorized taking may compromise trust |
| Confront guards | Social approach, lawful challenge or source-backed combat if provoked | Directness | Detention, retreat, report or violence; Level 1 fight not presumed fair |

These are **adventure-local suggested targets**, not universal rules for locks, roofs or social tasks. If the group has unpressured access and failures carry no cost, canonical Take 10/Take 20 or automatic resolution may apply. Do **not** roll until a method and its plausible failure consequences are clear. A successful forced entry doesn't magically remove witnesses or lock damage; a failed quiet attempt doesn't automatically shatter the mechanism.

## Area B — Receiving hall: the six-versus-four puzzle

> Under a hanging scale, the clerk has spread two papers beneath a brass weight. A chalk board says six crates were expected. Four dark rectangles on the stone show where boxes recently stood. Across the newest seals, someone has written *under inspection* in bright ink.

**Give players Handouts D-1 and D-2** from [player-handouts.md](player-handouts.md) as physically inspected records. **D-1** lists all six outbound crate marks; **D-2** lists only the four received crate marks, with no printed solution. **D-3**, a water-damaged transfer slip, can be found in the office, offered by the porter, or recovered from a duplicate; it explicitly records 03 and 05 left at Old Kiln Rest. Let the players compare those documents rather than announcing the missing numbers first.

**GM solution:** 17-01, 17-02, 17-04, 17-06 returned. 17-03 and 17-05 stayed at Old Kiln Rest. Returned crates were not universally dry: some bundles show saturated wrapping under replacement labels. The public expression “held for inspection” withheld this meaningful loss. Comparing evidence reveals no proof of theft.

**Methods:** Direct player reasoning from the printed numbers; Intellect with relevant trade/accounting knowledge if contextual interpretation is needed; Awareness to locate original seal impressions; asking the porter for handling history; a request for duplicate originals. A trained character may recognize normal inventory conventions without rolling. **Never require a single success to let the party see the figures.**

**Fail-forward:** An attempted accelerated inspection can scatter or damage a working copy, forcing consultation of the clerk or surviving original and attracting attention; the numbers are still reconstructible elsewhere. A player who guesses “theft” can bring that hypothesis to NPCs without being told what to think.

## Area C — Wet storage: the precarious stack

> The room smells of damp wood and grayleaf. Two towers of crates lean toward one another, held apart by a makeshift brace. Beneath the top boards, someone has folded newer paper around older stained wrapping.

**Unstable stack:** A cautious party may simply work around it; safely move crates with time and helpers; brace it with Carpentry; examine load paths using Intellect + Engineering; or cross a narrow gap with a genuinely applicable Agility + Acrobatics check. Suggested **12** for ordinary risk, **15** for rushed precarious handling. If movement becomes unsafe, describe the specific danger before resolving it. A collapsed stack can block an aisle, fracture a crate or alert guards, not retroactively erase every clue.

**Spoiled sample:** Under seal 17-04 a wrapped grayleaf sample remains discolored. It is **not** suitable for distribution without skilled assessment. Intellect + Medicine or Herbalism **12** may help determine specific physical spoilage and what safe handling requires; ordinary visible dampness is automatic. Proper labeling and delivery to Mira can produce an authenticated sample and earn trust.

## Area D — Clerk's office: people preserve evidence

The anxious **depot clerk** has the annotated receiving register and original diversion copy. The **porter** remembers that the wet crates arrived after the road detour, and the cargo lift has not functioned properly since. Both fear being blamed for managerial decisions.

**Conversation options:** Request inspection as a service to Mira; present Edda's public entry; explain the numbers from B; offer to repair the lift; threaten the clerk (which may make him refuse to sign); or search without permission (which may later raise provenance questions). The clerk is not a mastermind or automatic obstacle. He can authenticate the duplicate signed `R.C.` and the existence of a separate reserve voucher in the ordinary vault.

**Jammed cargo lift:** The lifting mechanism stops at an intermediate height; a warped retaining pin is accessible from a service platform. Intellect + Engineering **15** can diagnose and safely release it if uncertain; Carpentry and appropriate tools can replace the wooden pin; manual unloading or waiting for a trained operator works more slowly. A rushed forced effort may damage the lift or crush goods if the mechanics make that plausible. Repairing it can secure staff support for later loading at Ember Watch.

## Area E — Service bay and upper gallery

A loading shutter opens at scheduled intervals when staff are active. A narrow side stair leads to the upper store and window, which can be approached from the roof. A sheltered drain offers a route for small objects, not an unexamined full-body tunnel. Let players observe how the site works before committing to infiltration.

**Useful decisions:** Wait for a delivery; carry a covered bundle with genuine workers; cut through a damaged rope to create a diversion (and own the consequences); climb with a secured line; or retreat after patrols change. Nobody automatically “succeeds at stealth” for rolling high without a physically credible path.

## Area F — Ordinary records vault

A properly secured cabinet contains the route authorization and **an independent Ember Watch reserve voucher**. Its lock is difficult but does not require the secret archive. With authorized access, the clerk can open it. Under pressure, a plausible maintenance lock attempt is **Precision + Lockwork vs 15**; forcing the cabinet is noisy and risks damage to fragile documents. Players can instead bring Edda or ask the clerk for copies.

**The documents show:** Renn authorized the diversion; four crates were recorded as received; the clean Ember Watch reserve was catalogued separately and is not represented by the spoiled lot. Provide **D-4** from [player-handouts.md](player-handouts.md) when inspected or properly copied; its two distinct vault leaves may be revealed separately. It is an authentic authorization and reserve record, not a completed medicine release. Naeris remains entitled to verify a release; the voucher does not mean the party legally owns the medicine.

## Area G — The Three Obligations (optional secret archive)

The concealed shelf opens by an ordinary brass-disc mechanism. A weathered inscription reads:

> “Warm the hands that labor. Preserve the lives they serve. Keep account of what is entrusted.”

Three rotary discs bear multiple symbols. The first bears Fire, Stone, Wind and Storm; the second Life, Kinship, Death and Growth; the third Order, Liberty, Memory and Fate. **Solution: Fire → Life → Order.** The symbols commemorate voluntary duties and civic traditions. They do not cast spells, receive divine messages or require Veil access.

**Fair clue placement (all available here):**

1. **Plaque by the hearth-shaped wall niche in B:** “The first warmth goes to those who worked.”
2. **Clerk's oath pinned in D:** “Preserve the lives the storehouse serves.”
3. **Restoration-plan margin in F:** “Account for what is entrusted; the last disc closes the book.”

The inscription itself gives much of the sequence. A player who understands it opens the door without rolling. Other paths: ask Edda about the old rebuilding; locate the renovation drawing; inspect visible machinery using Intellect + Engineering **15** when meaningful; pick the separate maintenance lock with Precision + Lockwork **18**; physically force an accessible panel with Strength **18** and leverage, accepting historic damage and noise. Wrong sequence: ordinary resistance, a visible latch reset and perhaps a click heard by staff; no magical damage or hard lockout.

**Optional finds:** (a) older **protected duplicate reserve voucher**, useful if the normal copy's origin is disputed; (b) historical emergency release procedure, describing recorded need, witnessed custody and proposed transport—not an automatic authorization; (c) a folded covered-route plan and a list of useful cargo fittings. Give **D-5** from [player-handouts.md](player-handouts.md), only if the optional protected archive is actually explored. Players can leave with full main-plot evidence without ever finding G.

### Guard response and social/combat boundary

Hale notices actual evidence of intrusion—broken padlock, sudden noise, an unauthorized figure—not an abstract failure count. He initially orders a stop and asks for an explanation. If the players comply, he can detain them, escort them out or call Edda; physical escalation depends on what characters do. If deliberate attack begins, use only his existing **Ordinary Human Soldier B06-EN-02** source profile and legal turn/defense rules; the civilian watchkeeper's action is to seek safety or alarm, not a made-up monster attack. The benchmark is unrated for a Level 1 party. A PC's capture, surrender and negotiation are legitimate directions for the story; avoid forced lethal combat.

### Closure and carry-forward

Give the characters a chance to seal, attest or transcribe the evidence. Note whether the clerk and porter corroborate it; whether Hale trusts the party; whether the depot is broken into; whether the lift works; whether the secret archive remains intact; and what physical items the party carries. If the party leaves with little, the market ledger and cavern dispatch remain available. A lawful inspection can be more effective than a heist, while a well-executed covert entry can still reveal real evidence despite legal consequences.

**Closing description:**

> The depot doors close behind you. It is still the same damp building, holding the same damaged goods. But the numbers no longer fit the notice in the square, and someone has put a name to the choice that sent them into the rain.

**Milestone One candidate (if this is the first substantial branch achievement):** Preserving an actionable reconciliation, authentic source evidence or a genuinely useful depot-based response may qualify as **The First Breakthrough** if the GM declares and records it. Opening the optional secret room is not necessary.

**Handouts:** D-1 outbound manifest, D-2 receiving ledger, D-3 damaged transfer slip, D-4 vault voucher, D-5 protected archive notes. **Next sites:** Market, Cavern and Ember Watch remain reachable regardless of where this chapter appeared in the party's order.

# THE OLD ROAD CAVERN
## Independent middle chapter · Combat and exploration · GM version 1

**Run at any point after Session 1, even if the characters have not met a merchant witness or entered the depot.** This branch has a genuine tactical wildlife threat, a rescued-dispatch objective and an **optional** major boss. The main mystery is not locked behind a monster kill, poison resistance or magical technique. Target approximately 3–4 hours, adjusted to actual character level, risk tolerance and chosen objectives.

### At a glance

**Objective:** Find the old layover, establish what physically happened to Lot 17, recover the sealed case, and determine whether the route is safe enough for relief transport. **Guaranteed facts:** real rain exposure, separate crates 17-03/05, Renn's authorized diversion, existence of the dry Ember Watch reserve. **Wildlife:** warning signs of the Hunter in surrounding woods; lesser mundane cave spiders occupying the accessible cavern; **Veil-touched Dire Giant Spider** in the elective Heartweb Chamber. **Optional person:** a salvage worker trapped above the lower gallery. **Optional rewards:** exceptional silk, a former courier's strongbox, rescue-related aid, useful transport materials. **Core passage:** never requires defeating the dire spider.

### Read aloud: leaving the maintained road

> The old road is little more than two parallel ruts through roots and washed stone. Here a wheel has cut a deep crescent in the mud; there a ditch has been repaired with branches. A broken timber sign points toward Old Kiln Rest. Behind the trees, something large has pushed aside fallen brush and left a gouge high on a trunk.
>
> Beyond the next turn, the roofless walls of the relay appear against a limestone hillside. The doorway of a shallow cavern sits above the abandoned loading yard.

Ask who scouts, whether the group moves openly or cautiously, and what gear they brought for a wet, rocky road. The road sketch from Session 1 is sufficient to reach this place. Awareness can reveal useful signs and Tracking can explain them, but characters see obvious broken timber, rut patterns and an open trail without rolling.

## Points of interest and routes

 Public old road
      |
 [A] Old Kiln Rest / cargo remains ---- [B] Woodline & Hunter signs
      |
 [C] Limestone cave mouth
      |
      +-------------------+
      |                   |

      |                   |

The location is traversable through at least two routes around the main chamber. Create a tactical map from actual geometry before running a fight; this sketch provides relationships, not measured combat squares or automatic line-of-sight.

## Area A — Old Kiln Rest: physical proof

> What remains of the relay roof is caught in nettles beside the rear wall. Inside, shallow wooden skids mark where a wagon was once unloaded. A grayleaf bundle has burst under a rock; the leaves are dark and matted, their paper wrapping softened by old rain.

**Facts available on inspection:** The shelter does not keep out rain. Old wagon tracks and damaged lashings align with a hurried stop. Remaining crate fragments bear **17-03** and **17-05**. The condition of the material supports **ordinary water spoilage**, not magical corruption. A person with Herbalism/Medicine knowledge may identify degrees of spoilage and safe sample handling; a player can still see that this cargo was wet.

**Choices:** Take labeled fragments or a sealed sample; draw the scene; compare it with the depot's manifest if the party has it; stabilize the shelter; search for signs of recent human visitors; return to the village with this evidence alone. If the party leaves now, the cave remains accessible later, and the market/depot can independently confirm the case.

**Optional ordinary find:** Frayed but usable rope, an oilcloth section and unbroken wooden cargo fittings. The amount and carrying load must make fictional sense; they are not free equipment-generation powers.

## Area B — The woodline: the Hunter is not a boss trigger

The **Hunter of the Woods** inhabits nearby territory rather than waiting for players in a designated combat chamber. Signs may include deep claw or horn marks, fresh animal remains, displaced roots, unusually quiet browsing animals and large tracks. Use Awareness for things the characters might miss and Tracking to interpret age or direction. No single check determines whether the Hunter attacks. A party that respects the evidence can plan a wide route, turn back, distract without harm or prepare a guarded approach.

If the Hunter is actually present, follow its **existing B06-EN-03 source-backed fixture and ecology**, including intimidation, territory defense and potential withdrawal. It is an **unrated elite predator** and a likely severe threat to beginning adventurers; do not force an unavoidable ambush to secure the dispatch. Separate its territorial behavior from the spiders' cave behavior. Retreat and observing without combat count as genuine successes.

**Read aloud (if close signs are found):**

> One tree carries a fresh gouge at chest height, with splinters curled outward. The prints below it are not from a wagon animal. Whatever made them was heavy enough to sink into ground that held your own tracks.

## Area C — The cave mouth: telegraph the spiders

The mouth opens into cool air. Thin threads of ordinary silk connect the lower stones, and insect husks accumulate beneath an overhang. The party can recognize this as occupied wildlife habitat by looking carefully. Give them space to choose lighting, formation, withdrawal and an approach route.

An ordinary nonmagical spider may flee light or vibration while another defends its nest; no automatic synchronized squad tactics. If the characters block exits, burn silk or make loud repeated disturbances, the creatures may react according to what is actually happening. Ordinary rock walls do not become magical traps.

## Area D — Upper ledge: approach and rescue

A narrow natural ledge overlooks the webbed gallery. With appropriate climbing gear or cautious footing it can provide a route to the abandoned refuge without entering the lower floor. Under genuinely hazardous circumstances use the existing Traversal framework—often Agility + Climbing for technical movement or Strength + Climbing for hauling—with an adventure-local suggested target **12** for an accessible but wet ledge, **15** for a rushed exposed crossing. Never substitute a die roll for physically impossible leaps.

**Optional NPC:** **Nell Wren**, a local salvage worker, entered earlier the same day to recover old wagon hardware and has become isolated on the ledge. Nell has no combat equipment or invented attacks. She is frightened and can speak softly if the party approaches. Rescue can involve a rope, safe descent, a distraction or arranging escort; she might volunteer what she saw: a sealed case in F and larger webs farther down. Do not make her testimony the only clue to either.

**Choice under threat:** Rescue Nell immediately, secure the case and return, or retreat to bring help. If ignored, her plight may remain unresolved and have ordinary consequences, but the GM need not kill her at an arbitrary session boundary.

## Area E — Webbed gallery: the principal combat opportunity

> The floor opens into a chamber of uneven limestone, with dark pillars and a low slope. Web strands hang between the stones, thickest around a narrow defile. From above comes the faint rasp of legs moving across rock.

**Tactical features:** pillars provide directional cover, narrow gaps reward formation, a raised ledge is accessible with effort, and natural routes create alternative ways to the refuge. Put spider signs on the map before drawing initiative. Players may fight, lure creatures away, cross cautiously, circle by the ledge, use legal ranged attacks or withdraw. The objective is **access to F and safe return**, not exterminating wildlife.

**Mundane cave spiders:** New encounter creatures whose complete combat statistics are **not yet authored**. Their intended behavior is ambush and local defense; they are not automatically venomous, Veil-empowered or able to cast spells. **Do not fill in missing bite, AP, web or defensive values by borrowing the dire spider profile.** If running before those profiles are written, narrate a noncombat bypass or use only properly authored and disclosed substitution; treat the tactical combat slot as a production gate, not an independently certified playtest.

**Challenge adaptation:** For Level 1, emphasize warning, ways around and quick retreat. At higher levels the GM may stage a more complex but still uncalibrated encounter once creature profiles exist. Never raise creature HP solely because this branch happens third.

## Area F — Abandoned refuge: the essential case

This former wagon shelter is reached from the upper ledge or lower gallery. Behind a dry limestone lip is a **sealed dispatch case**, abandoned during the rain-diversion confusion and later overlooked. It is **not in the Heartweb Chamber**. Give the player **C-1 Dispatch note** from [player-handouts.md](player-handouts.md) upon finding and opening the case by ordinary means. A broken seal or difficult lid changes provenance or time but not the fact that the case exists.

**Contents:** a dated route order signed for Renn Calder (`R.C.`), note of crates **17-03/17-05** left at Old Kiln Rest, confirmation that four crates went onward, and a reference to the **separate dry grayleaf reserve at Ember Watch**. The dispatch does not state that Renn concealed public loss—that is inferred when compared with the posted notice or a witness and later established through his response.

**Optional second note — C-4:** A folded, unsent message in the abandoned-refuge dispatch copies says Talla took Mira's request toward Ember Watch. Give **C-4** only if the players find that note. She writes that she **kept the family ring with her**; the physical ring remains with Talla at Ember Watch unless the players' actual actions change custody. The note indicates a destination, not guaranteed safe arrival or supernatural intervention.

**Alternative access/failure:** The case may be retrieved by reaching F through either route; it can be partially wet and its seal challenged, but readable basic contents survive unless players deliberately destroy it. An NPC witness, depot order and Edda's record independently support the same facts. A party may leave with the case without fighting either spider species.

## Area G — Heartweb threshold: a clear voluntary decision

> The rock narrows again. A pale cable of silk crosses the passage, far thicker than anything at the cave mouth. Beyond it, faint light catches the chitin of something enormous clinging to a high wall. The stone beneath bears old scrapes where heavy cargo was dragged through long ago.

Make the danger **unmistakable** and allow retreat before combat. If the party has already obtained the case, tell them plainly through the environment that the path ahead is **not necessary to leave**. They may mark the entrance, return later, attempt a stealthy salvage or challenge the creature. It has no need to guard a mandatory map key.

## Area H — The Heartweb Chamber (optional boss)

A broad cavern holds hanging silk, vertical limestone, stone shelves, narrow cover positions and the nest of one **Veil-touched Dire Giant Spider (B06-EN-04)**. This is a rare biological predator with substantial inward Veil-supported recovery. It is **more aggressive** than mundane cave spiders and prefers to attack one target without letting allies interfere. It does **not** know the plot, cast spells or spend Vigor to attack.

### Locked design baseline, pending full creature implementation

- **Body:** large aggressive ambush predator; strong chitin, fangs, wall/ceiling climbing; proposed 1×2 footprint; animal-like cognition.
- **Resilience:** proposed HP **16**, Vigor **64**, natural Armor **3**, natural regeneration **5 HP per real own-turn Regeneration Step** while HP/Vigor and repair restrictions permit; **1 Vigor per HP**, no Vigor burn; no spell VP or player Focus.
- **Fangs:** proposed **d20+6**, **6+d8 Piercing, Pen 2**, one paid **24-AP** Physical Action; the printed damage already includes creature physical contribution, do not add Strength again. Normal successful hit can exceed an ordinary sword's damage and a target's single regeneration step.
- **Venom:** use **TX-MY**, existing **Minor DC10 injection myotoxin**, only after an actual surviving Piercing puncture; Full **-2 ordinary MP and -1 real Strength muscular exertion checks for 6 affected-turn intervals**, Reduced same for **3**, following the existing one-toxin-instance policy. No paralysis or free extra poison packet.
- **Web:** physically projected short-range adhesive shot at **3 squares**, **two prepared shots** (limited gland supply; no Vigor or VP spent), **separate paid action**, intended to anchor one target's movement until freed. Final attack-defense, binding, removal and action details **not yet authored**. Do not equate with universal Grappled/Restrained, invent damage or add free attacks.
- **Climbing:** can use suitable walls/ceilings through anatomical adaptation. Final creature-specific vertical MP and traversing support under the Traversal foundation are **not yet authored**. It cannot bite through stone, ignore cover or teleport.
- **Turn benchmark:** proposed **42 Turn AP** with **24 AP** for Bite or Web Shot; final source-backed stat block and tests still required.

**Hunting priorities:** (1) choose peripheral prey, (2) web a companion who could assist, (3) move above or around approaches so it cannot be surrounded, (4) descend for a heavy puncturing bite, (5) move back to vertical terrain when pressure rises. Its instincts notice proximity and movement; do not grant tactical mind-reading or free knowledge of PC Archetypes. Each web, fang and climb requires real legal timing and movement. It cannot automatically do all in one turn.

**Player counterplay:** Stay together; free the anchored ally by physically cutting or removing silk under the eventual authored procedure; use stone pillars and full cover to deny a direct web shot; reach upper ledges by routes made clear beforehand; deny the spider the exposed prey it wants; concentrate legal attacks and wear down finite regenerative reserve; leave when risk outweighs reward. A spider that cannot reach prey must move into danger or abandon pursuit rather than attacking through walls.

**Map design requirement:** The eventual tactical chamber must have reachable cover, connected ledges and at least one secure withdrawal lane. It must not put every PC in an unavoidable isolated box under a ceiling ambush. If the creature's real ranged-defense and climbing mechanics are not ready, **do not claim the fight mechanically runnable**; play the exploration and leave H as a future-encounter location rather than inventing magic or actions.

### Optional Heartweb rewards

1. **Dire Spider Silk:** rare, unusually resilient raw biological textile. Careful accessible salvage may yield a modest coil; safe control of the chamber might permit more. It is useful trade stock or material for **ordinary cargo-binding experiments**, not an automatic enchanted item or numeric Strength/Guard bonus. Pricing/crafting is subject to later equipment decisions.
2. **Lost Courier's Strongbox:** an old, unrelated merchant courier's box caught near the nest—not Talla's property. Inside are sound cargo straps, waterproof wrapping, small repair fittings, and **a modest purse of 6 silver pieces** as proposed adventure-local salvage. This can help with the emergency freight effort or be kept; normal ownership and local custom may matter.
3. **An unusual discovery:** the spider's presence is rare enough that a careful observer can bring credible descriptions to scholars later. No invented free skill rank, XP or instant proof of Veil corruption of the shipment.

**Different outcomes:** Defeat/drive away = more secure access to salvage; stealth = possible strongbox or a smaller accessible silk coil; negotiation is not ordinary language with an animal, but bait, distraction and territorial avoidance are possible; retreat = the creature lives, the party keeps the dispatch and the story continues. The optional boss never governs release of Ember Watch stock.

## Closing the cavern branch

Ask the party whether it has identified a return route suitable for **people, pack animals or a loaded wagon**—these may be different answers. Repaired old road sections, Hunter territory, steep ledges and ruined bridge approaches all matter. The group might mark dangers, arrange a guarded detour, enlist Nell as guide or report hazards to Hale. Consequences carry into the finale without demanding a single preferred solution.

> The last light on the old road finds the broken relay exactly where the storm left it. You have evidence that the grayleaf was lost here, not stolen into some hidden sorcerer's store. Whether the route can serve a living village again is a different question—and one you may still have to answer.

**Milestone One candidate (if this is the first substantial branch achievement):** Recovering the dispatch and useful independent field/route evidence, or combining an equivalent rescue and relief-enabling plan, may qualify as **The First Breakthrough** on GM declaration. Merely defeating an animal or visiting the optional Heartweb Chamber does not. The cavern remains playable at Level 1 without confronting the dire spider.

### If the party comes in a different order

- **First:** Rain marks, case, route and reserve reference stand alone. No previous evidence is needed.
- **After market:** Jorin's account may help locate the side path or distinguish recorded from actual wagon positions. Still show the physical scene.
- **After depot:** Numbers 03/05 can be matched against the crate fragments, strengthening provenance and the later petition.
- **After early Ember Watch:** The route may now be explored expressly for safe freight and the case for public accountability; the motivation changes without making the branch redundant.

**GM closure notes:** dispatch recovered/condition; **C-1 case, C-2 field sketch, C-3 optional salvage inventory, and C-4 optional unsent note** revealed only as actually earned; route assessed for foot and loaded transport separately; Nell rescued or left; mundane spiders disturbed; Heartweb approached/avoided/resolved; Hunter signs/contact; actual wounds, Vigor, VP and time; optional equipment or silk recovered. **Handouts:** **C-1** sealed dispatch when opened; **C-2** old-road field sketch when the party finds or makes a reliable route sketch; **C-3** optional older courier strongbox inventory only if the box is actually recovered; **C-4** optional Talla note only when discovered in the refuge copies. All four are individual sections in [player-handouts.md](player-handouts.md).

# EMBER WATCH AND THE RECKONING
## Session 5 · Integrated finale · GM version 1

**This is the intended concluding session, but Ember Watch is a real location the party can visit earlier.** If a partial release or direct petition already occurred, begin with the outstanding distribution, transport and accountability decisions rather than undoing earlier success. Target roughly 3–4 hours, with social, practical and potentially physical confrontation integrated according to decisions from the three branches.

### Finale promise

The party must decide **who gets safe medicine, how it reaches them, who can verify the account, and what responsibility Renn bears**. None of these is solved solely by defeating Naeris or discovering one hidden token. The climax can be tense without turning a legitimate priest and steward into the final villain. Combat or protective tactics are possible, but unnecessary violence should not be structurally rewarded.

### Pre-finale milestone check

The intended starting state is **Level 3**, provided the GM has recognized and recorded **two different substantive group achievements**: (1) a meaningful first investigation breakthrough, usually after Session 2, and (2) an actionable plan for relief and transportation, usually after Session 4. Neither award is automatic from having completed the session or all three chapters. If an early Ember Watch visit or an unforeseen plan changes the order, award by achievement at a settled point; never fabricate a missing event to hit a numbered session. Do not grant free VP/Vigor restoration, erase wounds or reset existing timers on a level award.

Before opening the finale, make a quick practical assessment: **Who will verify the emergency need? What can Naeris release responsibly? How will the sound parcels be covered, carried, protected and recorded on arrival?** The party can form this plan without all three investigations; fewer earned aids simply mean more practical problems to resolve here.

### Prepare from the branch ledger

Before running, mark:

- **Evidence:** Edda-certified ledger, physical depot manifest, sealed cavern dispatch, signed witness statements, authentic reserve voucher, Mira's sample report.
- **People:** Edda and Renn's commitments, witness willingness, Hale's trust, depot workers or Nell available, Talla's status and any early meeting with Naeris.
- **Logistics:** carts/animals, covered material, lift repairs, travel route for foot versus wagon, time of day/weather, injury/VP/Vigor, Hunter territory and road hazards.
- **Truth disclosed:** public notice corrected, private restitution promised, rumors unchallenged, harmed or accused innocents.
- **Optional:** Heartweb silk/equipment, strongbox proceeds. A successful or failed optional boss has no special permission to release the reserve.

Keep the specific consequences already played. Do not insert a new attack simply because this is Session 5.

## Scene F1 — At the watch: a finite reserve and several responsibilities

> Ember Watch rises from the wet road as a square stone storehouse beside a low hearth. The door bears the marks of generations of repairs. A small line of people waits under the overhang, not for a miracle but for measured parcels of food, tools and medicine.
>
> A priest in a plain traveling cloak checks the last entry in a ledger before looking up. “I'm Naeris. If you've come for grayleaf, tell me what has happened in Dunmere—and how you plan to bring any relief safely home.”

**Naeris's values:** Fire means warmth, shelter and responsibility to those served; it does not tell her to hoard medicine. She fears a large, undocumented release will cause harm to other households later or let unsafe and safe goods be confused. She recognizes urgency and will make a rational proportional decision. **Talla** is resting in a side room after a difficult journey and ankle injury. She carries Mira's inactive signature-bearing ring. A quiet meeting with her can satisfy Mira's personal stake regardless of the broader negotiation.

**Concrete inventory for the adventure:** Naeris has **eight sealed dry parcels** of grayleaf in this emergency reserve. **Two** are already assigned to immediate needs in communities served by Ember Watch; **six** are presently unassigned but accounted for. Mira reports that **four sound parcels** would meet Dunmere's immediate emergency needs while wider resupply is arranged. These are **narrative parcel counts**, not a canonical dose/economy or mass-healing formula. Players may negotiate a smaller or larger justified release, split deliveries, or offer to replace the used reserve.

**Important early-arrival policy:** If the PCs arrive with no paperwork but credible testimony and urgent need, Naeris can offer **up to two provisional parcels** for supervised immediate delivery and arrange a fuller decision after evidence or witnesses arrive. She does not demand completion of three adventures. The remaining release hinges on practical verification and competing duties, not a hard quest flag.

### Roleplaying the petition

| Player approach | Naeris's plausible response | Follow-through |
|---|---|---|
| Edda's certified account and specific patients' need | Welcomes an auditable request | Offers release matching urgency and available allocation |
| Depot sample/record | Asks who authenticated it; checks the seal | Can verify integrity with Talla, clerk, Edda or record comparison |
| Cavern dispatch only | Accepts its relevance but notes damage may be incomplete | Seeks a second witness or careful distribution plan; no blanket refusal |
| Honest appeal without documents | Takes need seriously, considers limited supervised release | A courier or witness can attest while party prepares transport |
| Renn signs and admits concealment | Can accept restitution and accountability plan | May require independent copy so records do not vanish again |
| Threat or attempted seizure | Warns that other people also rely on the reserve | Hale or staff may intervene if present; negotiation, retreat or source-backed combat consequences |

**Do not require a universal Presence check.** Many sufficiently grounded proposals should work without one. A genuinely uncertain request can use Presence with circumstances or Intellect with appropriate narrow record/legal expertise. For example, arguing a realistic distribution and receipt scheme may be more persuasive than a fiery speech. No divine voice dictates a die result.

### Talla and Mira's ring

Talla confirms when she left, what she witnessed and that the dry reserve is a separate cache. She is **not secretly an enemy and not held prisoner**. Her ankle is strained from the return road; ordinary care is meaningful. If the players ask about her ring, she describes it as Mira's heirloom and identifies its inactive Life / Restoration signature by family story. A lawful **Veil Inspection**, if a PC has the ring in hand and spends its normal resource/action cost, can identify the observable signature but **cannot explain the convoy, cure grayleaf or reveal item history**. Talla may carry a reply to her mother if the party offers a safe return.

**Read aloud (if Talla meets the party):**

> The young courier rises too fast and winces before she takes a careful seat again. “Mira sent me because she was afraid the notice was wrong. Naeris heard me. I thought I could bring an answer home myself.” She grips the small ring threaded on a cord. “Tell her I'm coming.”

## Scene F2 — The release plan: a social and practical puzzle

Present the players with a clear challenge, **not one correct code**:

- Four parcels are urgently useful in Dunmere.
- Two of the eight at Ember Watch are assigned elsewhere.
- Six are available for accountable emergency allocation.
- The old road may be damaged or inside Hunter territory; another, longer route exists through the maintained watch road.
- At least one dry, stable method of transporting the parcels is needed.

Ask the group to decide: **How many parcels now? Who certifies the handoff? Who loads and escorts them? Which road is used? Who in Dunmere distributes them and documents the result?** They may answer with witnesses, a sealed release receipt, tarps, carts, pack animals, a staged journey, a mixed route or an urgent smaller first delivery.

**Success is a workable plan, not a particular item check:** An authentic receipt plus viable covered transport is one solution. Public Edda certification may be replaced by two independent witnesses if trustworthy. Salvage equipment can substitute for purchased straps. Renn's wagon is useful but not mandatory. Mira can confirm which patients need care but cannot retroactively make spoiled medicine safe.

**Give Handout F-1** from [player-handouts.md](player-handouts.md) as a **blank** release-and-custody form; complete quantities, signatures and transport only according to the actual decision and handover. **F-2** is the optional blank Dunmere public correction after delivery; **F-3** is an optional Naeris letter template, not a pre-committed award. No numerical logistics subsystem or forced group check is created.

### Meaningful consequences, not generic failure

| Plan problem | What actually happens | How PCs can respond |
|---|---|---|
| No dry covering | Exposed medicine risks spoilage if rain returns | Acquire oilcloth, split load, delay for safe weather, pay a carrier |
| No trustworthy receipt | Naeris worries about later denial of custody | Ask Talla/Edda/another witness; agree to witnessed handoff |
| Old road not fit for wagon | Heavy cart cannot pass known damaged section | Use maintained route, pack animals, smaller loads or repair a narrow section |
| Hunter territory unaddressed | Route through its hunting grounds is uncertain | Observe/widen detour, change transport method, travel at a safer opportunity |
| Village distrusts Renn | His authorization alone may lack credibility | Use Edda/Mira and independent record rather than inventing a social number |

If the players have already won provisional relief, this scene resolves how to get the **remaining justified supply** moving.

## Scene F3 — The journey home: integrated action and possible violence

Use a physically legible transport obstacle. A stone culvert on the **watch road** has shifted after rain: narrow passage for a cart, firm footpath beside it, muddy turn, two trees that can anchor a line. It does not require an invented movement hazard or compulsory combat. Explain the footing before checks.

**Read aloud:**

> Halfway down the watch road, the lead cart stops. The small stone culvert is not broken all the way across, but the wheel track at its edge has slumped into wet earth. A person can walk past it. Whether a loaded cart can do so is another matter. Behind you, the covered parcels wait under their straps.

**Approaches:** unload and hand-carry; replace a washed pin using Carpentry; assess load distribution with Intellect + Engineering (suggested **12** where uncertainty matters, **15** if hurried); secure ropes; use a smaller vehicle; retreat and take a wider path; build a temporary support. Relevant Climbing or Strength can matter with a real hauling method. Failure costs time, strains a rope, forces unloading or risks an actual fall/load loss—never a universal hidden penalty or automatically destroyed entire reserve.

### Optional confrontation: protect people, not just defeat opponents

Choose **at most one** escalation when earned by earlier events:

**A. Desperate claim at a road halt:** A family from a neighboring hamlet, fearing its own shortage, seeks an immediate share. A hotheaded armed escort may threaten to take a parcel if the party refuses any discussion. The party can explain the signed allocation, promise a witnessed share, escort the family to Naeris or retreat. If the escort genuinely attacks, the only available existing human benchmark is the **Ordinary Human Soldier B06-EN-02**, uncalibrated for this specific party; do not automatically assign that profile to a civilian or presume a balanced fight.

**B. Territorial Hunter crossing:** If the party knowingly uses the old-road route through warned territory, signs of the Hunter appear. It may stalk, display, retreat or attack depending on actual circumstance. A decision to go around, leave prey alone, maintain a compact formation or withdraw is a real outcome. If hostility develops, use its source-backed **B06-EN-03** fixture and openly treat the uncalibrated danger seriously.

**C. No hostile confrontation:** If players resolve transportation carefully and the route is safe, honor that. The climactic tension then lies in the distribution and reckoning. Do not fabricate brigands to guarantee a fight. If the table wants tactical stakes, play load protection, guarded marching, threatened lanes and actual terrain choices without pretending a noncombat skill scene is an attack sequence.

**Tactical preparation:** A relevant encounter map needs real cart/wagon positions, cover, an exit/retreat path and what threatens whom. Protection of medicine and people is the objective. Combat does not grant free transport success, and negotiations do not require a victory roll against an arbitrary morale meter.

## Scene F4 — Dunmere receives the medicine

> The first person to see the covered wagon from the market gate does not cheer. She runs to the healer's room. Soon the square is full of people trying not to crowd the parcels. Mira waits until the seals are checked before she permits anyone to open them.

**Distribution priorities:** Mira evaluates what has arrived; spoiled cargo remains quarantined; Edda (or another accepted witness) records parcels and recipients; voluntary workers help carry them. If fewer than four usable parcels arrive, the shortage continues but some immediate care can still improve. If none arrive, the PCs still have evidence and options for further appeals or resupply. No parcel is a universal HP item and no mass Restoration spell is implied.

Ask: “Who oversees the distribution? Who is present when the seal breaks? What will the notice in the square say tomorrow?” The answers decide how a material success becomes a social one.

## Scene F5 — Reckoning with Renn

The exact form depends on earlier choices. Use a witnessed hearing, a private meeting with Edda and Mira, or a later formal account. **Renn cannot undo the damage** by saying he meant well. He can provide repair labor, repay material losses where feasible, resign, be suspended, undergo a community process or remain in post under transparent oversight—subject to what authority Dunmere actually has. The players have influence through their evidence and relationships, **not sovereign legal power by default**.

**Read aloud (public hearing version):**

> Edda lays out the papers, starting with the original six-crate manifest. Mira says how many patients can be treated now, and how many still wait. Renn is asked what he knew when he let the notice stand. For the first time, there is no crate, road, or storm between the question and his answer.

**Three genuine tensions:** Edda needs an accurate record that survives; Mira needs time and cooperation for patients; the villagers need an accountable distribution system rather than merely a scapegoat. Encourage players to propose a remedy and hear consequences rather than choose from a morality slider.

### Ending families

| Ending | Conditions / fiction | Final image |
|---|---|---|
| **Open accounting and relief** | Supplies arrive safely; ledger corrected; actual responsibility acknowledged | People queue for safe care while an honest record is posted |
| **Private restitution, uneasy peace** | Relief delivered; Renn cooperates but public story remains partial | The village benefits now; Edda holds an unresolved promise to be answered |
| **Partial relief, costly journey** | Some supplies damaged/delayed or trust fractured | Mira triages; roads and future deliveries remain open work |
| **No immediate relief** | Request fails or transport fails through concrete choices/circumstances | The truth is known by some; the party has new options and unresolved responsibilities |

**No required villain.** Naeris may remain a dependable ally even after denying an irresponsible plan. The Hunter or dire spider may remain alive. A hearing may be postponed rather than forcibly decided by PCs with no mandate.

### Advancement, rewards, continuing play

The intended **L1→L2 First Breakthrough** and **L2→L3 Actionable Relief Expedition** awards occur earlier in the adventure as two independently GM-declared group-story achievements, usually after the first branch and just before this finale. Completing the finale does **not** supply a retroactive automatic level or repeat an earlier milestone. A further campaign-level award, if any, needs its own later source authority and declaration. Possible lasting rewards: Dunmere's hospitality, lawful transport work, witnesses who owe favors, restored trust, a modest agreed payment, the retained Heartweb salvage if recovered, a further mystery about regional supply routes, or a public commitment to rebuild Old Kiln Rest. These are fictional relationships and resources, not free universal mechanical bonuses.

### If the players never do one middle chapter

They can still reach the finale with one or two evidence paths or even a heartfelt direct request. Do not prevent relief because a chapter is unchecked. Instead write the missing corroboration into the problems they must solve: need a witness, document authority, find covered transport, or take more time to establish trust. **Every branch is an opportunity, not a permission flag.** The three reasons to investigate are distinct: secure cooperation and an honest account (Market), authenticate material proof and obtain tools (Depot), and establish the physical truth and safe freight route (Cavern). Their combined reward is a more credible petition, a better-equipped journey and a fairer reckoning—not a hidden three-clue door.

### Approved B06-EN-04 creature identity (design locked for the adventure)

- Veil-touched, unusually large, highly aggressive **biological ambush predator**; animal-like, not sapient, no spellcasting.
- Robust inward Veil integration = greater **Vigor reserve / regenerative capacity**, not Vigor-burning attacks or offensive Veil projection.
