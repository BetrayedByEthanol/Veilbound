# Mundane Toxins v0 — Exposure, Resistance, Effects and Catalogue

> **Executable biological/chemical-hazard proposal; ALL DCs, amounts, tick counts, duration and grading remain PROVISIONAL for playtesting.** This is a **mundane** toxin system: a real material must actually reach an eligible recipient by an authored exposure route. **Ordinary natural resistance reduces rather than fully negates the consequence**; an independently authored, applicable Veil protective effect may **negate** the eligible application under *that technique's exact permission, timing and budget*. This page does **not** author Venom Endurance, magical toxins, a generic Poisoned condition, toxin crafting, potion recipes, new source species or a medical antidote system.
>
> Authority and boundaries: [Core Resolution](../01-core-resolution/core-resolution.md), [Abilities](../02-character-creation/abilities-and-training.md), [Character turn order / ongoing hazards](initiative-and-round-structure.md#ongoing-hazards), [HP and Vigor](../06-vigor-wounds-death/hp-and-vigor.md), [Dying and Wounds](../06-vigor-wounds-death/dying-and-wounds.md), [Defensive Responses](defensive-responses-and-criticals.md), [Physical Hazards](physical-hazards-v0.md), [Basic Heal](../07-veil-magic/basic-heal-v0.md), [Vital Shelter](../07-veil-magic/vital-shelter-v0.md), and [Alchemy / Death domain boundaries](../../world/aspect-identities-and-boundaries.md#alchemy).

## 1. Scope and terminology

A **toxin family** describes the broad bodily function or tissue principally affected; **potency grade** describes the hazard's authored game severity. The family is **not** a mutually exclusive or complete scientific biochemical classification. Real venoms may have multiple mechanisms; an individual v0 profile has exactly **one** dominant family, one exposure route, one resistance DC, a full and a reduced consequence, and explicit timing/expiry. **Necrotoxin** here is a mundane fantasy label for *tissue-necrotizing toxicity*, not a supernatural Death/Plague effect. Cytotoxic and necrotizing behaviors can overlap in fiction; they remain separate **gameplay directions** in this provisional list.

- **Exposure**: The specified mundane material actually reaches the body through the specified route. Nearby presence, merely wielding a poisoned weapon, smelling something through a sealed mask, or being near a toxic plant **does not** cause automatic exposure.
- **Active toxin instance**: One successful exposure record (including a naturally resisted/reduced result), consisting of family/profile, grade, effect, remaining tick/duration clock, **a persistent 0–6-second short-clock remainder when applicable**, and any pending immediate residual marker. **One active mundane toxin instance per recipient** in v0, with the replacement policy below.
- **Effect**: HP injury, Vigor loss, a specific check/defense/MP penalty, or altered natural-regeneration/Vigor-Recovery processing as actually authored. There is **no universal Poisoned condition**, blanket disadvantage, automatic wound, or attribute-score change.
- **Damage versus active cause**: HP/Vigor damage already applied **persists under normal recovery rules** after toxin activity expires. Healing the injury does **not** remove the active toxin; stopping the toxin does not restore already lost HP/Vigor.
- **Severity**: **Minor** or **Moderate** for executable v0. **Serious/Severe toxin entries are deferred**; no automatic multiplication of these profiles to create lethal variants.

This system covers ordinary biological/chemical hazard effects regardless of whether their fictional source is plant, fungus, animal secretion, contamination or environmental exposure. **No actual ingredient harvesting, isolation, processing, weapon coating, dosing or production procedure is established**; encounter authors must independently establish that a specific exposure exists.

## 2. Exposure, check and application order

1. **Identify one authored source and profile:** The encounter/GM establishes a genuine material hazard and its profile **before** resolving exposure. Its route, consequence and grade are not retroactively inferred from a character's injury. A creature can be exposed by one or more routes only when that individual profile expressly allows them; no all-purpose poisoned attack, cloud or touch delivery is granted.
2. **Resolve physical delivery first:** 
   - **Injection:** a real puncture or other actual breached route into the body must be established by an existing attack, injury, creature ability or authored event. A weapon strike stopped by Guard, armor, lack of penetration, or an ordinary miss is **not an injection**. A positive HP-damaging puncture after mitigation is sufficient when exposure material is plausibly present; a no-HP puncture needs an **explicit independently established route** (e.g. already-open wound), never an implied armor bypass or second attack roll.
   - **Ingestion:** the recipient actually consumes the contaminated substance.
   - **Inhalation:** the recipient actually breathes an eligible airborne material through a viable exposure route; a sealed effective barrier prevents exposure.
   - **Contact:** the substance contacts a materially susceptible **exposed body surface**; the profile must allow contact. Appropriate protective clothing, gear or barriers may prevent contact. No automatic transdermal poisoning from touching any surface.
   Successful delivery **does not** automatically make the toxin effect unavoidable; it proceeds to natural resistance below. Armor, Cover, Guard and Dodge resolve normally where relevant to **delivery**, but ordinary Armor is **not subtracted again** from subsequent internal HP-damage ticks. Unless an effect specifically authors eligible typed resistance, ordinary Stone Skin/physical HP resistance cannot simply be presumed to cancel generic internal toxin injury.
3. **Nonstack gate:** If one ordinary toxin instance is already active, apply the [one-instance policy](#4-nonstacking-re-exposure-and-ending) *before* any toxin resistance roll. Ignored exposure never creates a fresh check, tick, duration, injury packet or reset. Prior exposure's already-applied losses are not undone.
4. **Independently applicable magical protection:** A *real, separately authored* preventive Veil technique may negate this application **only if its own effect actually says so**, with its own legal action, timing, resource, target and magnitude limit. A natural Vitality success **never** becomes perfect toxin immunity; this catalogue alone grants no Veil defense, free Reaction or automatic magic save. A protective technique may instead protect against individual later packets if *its own* authored scope permits that. **Venom Endurance is still unauthored.**
5. **One initial mundane resistance check:** On valid exposure, roll **`d20 + target Vitality + explicitly applicable modifiers ≥ toxin DC`**. Use an *active* Vitality resistance roll even for an unaware/asleep character that has actually been exposed; consciousness does not imply voluntary immunity. **No default Weapon Training, Herbalism, Medicine or Alchemy bonus** to bodily resistance; knowing the source is not the same as resisting its effects. The source may already be identifiable without a knowledge check; appropriate Medicine/Herbalism/Alchemy checks can diagnose a meaningful uncertainty under ordinary noncombat rules, but **do not** grant free antidotes or modify toxin DC by default. **Natural 1/20 are not automatic outcomes on this check** (canonical unresolved general noncombat critical rules do not create automatic poison immunity).
6. **Apply exactly the table's result:** A failure uses **Full**; a success uses **Reduced** (which is deliberately **not zero** in these eleven profiles). No additional per-turn resistance checks or hourly repeated saving throws. Record only the relevant timer and effect. **Onset** is at valid exposure for instantaneous/continuous effects unless the entry states delayed onset; recurring ticks begin at the entry's first eligible scheduled tick, never retroactively at the exposure event itself.
7. **Resolve actual consequences separately:** HP loss follows existing 0-HP crossing, Dying and wound rules; direct Vigor damage never transforms into HP damage; natural regeneration uses the victim's Vigor and its current throughput; specific penalties modify **only** named quantities (not underlying Abilities/derived characteristics). The protective/cure effect, if any, must be **specifically authored** to stop future ticks, remove an active instance, or restore damage.

### Difficulty and resistance grades

| Potency | Normal initial DC | On natural success | On failure |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Minor** | **10** | Profile's **Reduced** consequence | Profile's **Full** consequence |
| **Moderate** | **12** | Profile's **Reduced** consequence | Profile's **Full** consequence |
| **Serious+** | **Deferred** | Not a legal default upgrade | Not authored |

The profile's Reduced effect generally halves the **number of finite damaging/impairment ticks** rounding up, or halves **instantaneous integer harm** rounding up (e.g. 4 HP → 2 HP). For **durations** in rounds/hours, halve and round up. These are **design patterns, not a second universal halving calculation**: use the **literal Reduced entry** in the catalogue; never reduce it *again*. A one-point penalty is reduced by its **duration** rather than silently disappearing. A reduced result still counts as exposure and an active instance.

## 3. Ticks, timekeeping and healing

**Short toxin-local interval clock (not a regeneration entitlement).** Finite round/turn-style toxin profiles (damage, onset delays, regeneration-throughput penalties, continuous penalties and residual nonstack markers) store their remaining interval count and an exact **`phase_seconds` remainder `0 ≤ phase_seconds < 6`**, starting at **0** on valid exposure. A completed short interval advances **only the toxin's own counter and consequence schedule**. The six-second-equivalent mapping is **local to this toxin**, not a global duration of combat rounds and **never grants a free turn, natural HP regeneration, Repair Progress, Dying survival expenditure, AP, MP, Guard, Reaction or Vigor Recovery**.

**In initiative — unchanged native turn order:**
- At the affected creature's **next genuinely reached own-turn Step 2 → Step 3 pair after exposure**, **one** toxin-local interval completes. The **real**, independently scheduled [Regeneration/Dying Step 2](initiative-and-round-structure.md#2-regeneration-step) executes in full under its own rules **before** the toxin's Step 3 effect. Counting the poison interval does **not** add a second Step 2; the interval's nominal six seconds are credited **instead of**, not in addition to, hypothetical real combat seconds. Retain any fractional `phase_seconds` from earlier exploration.
- Short HP/Vigor toxin **damage ticks** execute in the affected creature's **Step 3**, after its real regeneration, subject to scheduled onset. **Onset-delay intervals count** even if there is no damage. A toxin applied after the Step 2/Step 3 pair has already occurred in the current turn does not apply a retroactive tick or penalty; its next eligible pair advances its clock.
- A short **natural-regeneration-throughput penalty** applies to the next eligible **real Step 2** and for exactly its authored number of Step 2 events; if the Step 2 already finished before toxin application, it cannot be modified afterward. Floor resulting throughput at **0**, without converting the penalty into HP or Vigor damage. **Exploration hazard-only checkpoints below do not count as regeneration steps**, so this penalty is neither consumed nor triggers regeneration there; the instance's elapsed-time duration still advances, which can cause it to expire outside combat unused.
- Continuous named penalties apply immediately upon exposure and through exactly the stated number of toxin-local intervals, including intervening genuine defense and movement. Expire after the last interval's Step 3 or its exploration equivalent, before any later check/defense. They do not change underlying Abilities or introduce an extra final round.
- HP injury from toxin ticks is **HP damage**, never a literal direct Vigor packet. When the **real Step 2** lawfully restores 1 HP it costs 1 Vigor within throughput; this repairs injury but does not neutralize the active toxin. Existing wound/0-HP crossing, Dying and death rules remain authoritative; poison never creates a second default Dying loss.

**Outside initiative — deterministic hazard-only progression, not extra turns:**
- When `Δt ≥ 0` actual elapsed seconds pass, add them to the active short toxin's **`phase_seconds`**. While `phase_seconds ≥ 6` and the short instance is still active, subtract **exactly 6**, advance **one** toxin-local interval, and resolve **only** the explicitly scheduled toxin consequence (including delay/expiry). **No virtual Step 2 exists:** do **not** grant HP regeneration, Vigor spending, wound Repair Progress, or a fresh Dying survival allocation from the toxin clock. This is deterministic regardless of whether the active toxin is harmful, harmless, a delay, or an instant-effect residual nonstack marker.
- **Chronology / Dying:** Resolve distinct HP or Vigor packets sequentially, never as a new lumped attack; after each packet apply normal HP ≤ 0 entry into Dying, the existing *first positive-to-nonpositive* wound rule, and HP ≤ −Max HP death threshold immediately. **Do not also generate the default −1 HP Dying loss at each toxin-local interval**: the existing default Dying loss is resolved during a **real own-turn Step 2**, not during exploration time advancement. Ordinary HP/Vigor recovery from an **independently authorized** source still works normally at its own actual time, including a legal external Basic Heal; merely possessing an active toxin never authorizes it. Unresolved **general out-of-initiative natural regeneration and Dying progression** is explicitly **deferred**, not inferred here.
- The GM may **present** all due short effects together during a relevant exploration time-advance checkpoint, normally within the next hour, but the **number and order of toxin packets are mandatory**. “One check in the next hour” means one **bookkeeping/reporting checkpoint**, not a new Vitality save, a single tick, or the ability to postpone an imminent actual injury past its due fictional time. If a scheduled harm would affect player decisions, Dying, death, a wound or an intervention, resolve that chronological intermediate state before moving further in time.
- **Initiative transitions and retained remainder:** At initiative entry resolve every complete elapsed exploration interval already due and **retain any sub-six-second remainder unchanged**. During each actual affected-creature turn, count **one** toxin interval at its real Step 2/3, leaving that remainder intact. When initiative ends, retain it again; the next actual exploration seconds continue from the same fractional value. Do not double count notional combat time as elapsed exploration seconds. Five 5-second exploration windows separated only by zero-time mode switches cause **four** toxin intervals plus **1 retained second**. In 5 seconds exploration → one real affected-creature turn → 1 second exploration, exactly **two** toxin intervals complete: the first uses the genuine turn's Step 2/3; the second resolves **toxin harm only**, without virtual regeneration.
- **Explicit bounded consequence:** A creature with **HP 8, Vigor 10**, a harmless three-interval **TX-CY residual marker**, and 18 seconds of exploration remains **HP 8 / Vigor 10** if no independent recovery occurs. The marker expires; it is **not** a source of three extra regeneration allocations. A hemotoxin's three remaining 1-HP packets over 18 uninterrupted exploration seconds take a healthy HP-10 creature to **HP 7** with **Vigor unchanged**, unless a separately authorized recovery or other event occurs between packets. By contrast, **genuine initiative turns** may permit normal regeneration at each real Step 2. **TX-02 watches this mode-dependent recovery consequence** until a separate, global exploration-regeneration rule is authored; toxin bookkeeping itself must never create that rule.

**Long toxin-local duration and pulse clock (TX-NP, TX-EN, TX-HE):**
- Long toxins store an **absolute exploration time to their next actual hourly pulse/expiry**, plus remaining pulse count and any separate expiry. **Outside initiative**, subtract actual elapsed in-world seconds; TX-NP deals exactly **1 Vigor damage at each completed 3600-second pulse**, repeating at most the entry's 2/4 pulses. TX-EN maintains its penalty for **7200/10800 elapsed seconds** (Reduced/Full), without extra hourly damage. TX-HE waits for the *already legitimate* next scheduled Vigor Recovery event inside its **21600-second upper window**, rather than inventing one or gaining an independent Vigor Recovery.
- **Exploration → initiative**, including entry during a partly elapsed hour: at first active initiative round **R**, take **`s` whole remaining seconds until each next event** (next hourly pulse/expiry). Use the conservative, technique-local **`q = floor(s/6)`** complete round intervals. If **`q=0`**, resolve the due toxin pulse or expiry **at the transition before combat actions** (not a bonus attack). Otherwise schedule it at **Round Begin R+q**. For example, **TX-NP with 1 second remaining** fires its next 1-Vigor packet at initiative entry; **TX-NP with 37 seconds remaining** pulses at **Round Begin R+6** (1 second conservatively discarded). TX-EN with 37 seconds until expiry ends at **Round Begin R+6**; if only 1 second remains, it ends immediately.
- **Exposure initially occurs during initiative:** If a *new* long profile is successfully applied in round **R** (whether before or after Round Begin), begin its ordinary immediate penalty if any, and schedule its **first future** long event from that round's label: **TX-NP next pulse Round Begin R+600**; **TX-EN Reduced expiry R+1200 / Full expiry R+1800**; **TX-HE six-hour maximum marker R+3600**. Do **not** claim that the current partial round contributed six real seconds or resolve a fresh hourly packet immediately upon ingestion. Later events follow the same boundary procedure. At initiative entry with a previously exposed toxin and `q=0`, resolve that due packet **at entry**, then schedule a remaining TX-NP next packet at **R+600**, not R+600+the originally discarded seconds.
- **While initiative continues:** Long toxin pulses and expiry resolve at their recorded **Round Begin**, under the canonical [Round Begin procedure](initiative-and-round-structure.md#round-begin), **before participants' turns**, not on a character's Step 3. Once TX-NP's scheduled pulse lands, decrement remaining pulses and schedule the next one **600 Round Begin intervals later**, e.g. R+6 → R+606; expire the profile when its final pulse resolves. For TX-EN, expire at its recorded boundary. This is an authored **6-rounds-per-36-seconds / 600-rounds-per-hour surrogate**, not a declaration that initiative rounds have a globally defined real-second length. Never deal an hourly toxin pulse every combat round.
- **Initiative → exploration:** If leaving during round **C** with future toxin event scheduled at **Round Begin E**, set **`n = max(0, E-C)`**. The incomplete current round receives **zero** additional time credit. Set next-event exploration countdown to **`s = 6 × max(0, n-1)` seconds**, and if **`s=0`**, resolve the event at transition. This explicit conservative conversion is consistent with other time-bounded authored techniques: it may shorten a duration at a transition, but **cannot extend a toxin or indefinitely postpone a pulse**, even with repeated transitions. After a pulse, apply the entry's next full 3600-second interval normally. If both an expiry and pulse are recorded, evaluate each event with its own next-event countdown and preserve order.
- **TX-HE / six-hour Recovery boundary:** TX-HE **never grants or accelerates Vigor Recovery**. If an **independently valid** 6-hour Recovery event and this profile's scheduled expiry coincide (including at a Round Begin), apply its −2/−1 modifier to that legitimate event **first**, then expire TX-HE. If no actual eligible Recovery occurs by its six-hour expiry, the marker ends harmlessly. Convert the **expiry marker**, not the entire global Vigor Recovery economy, with the same local `q` / `n` rules. No toxin-created recovery at R+3600.
- This long-duration conversion is intentionally **conservative**, like [Weaken Restraints](../07-veil-magic/weaken-restraints-v0.md) and [Woodland Rapport](../07-veil-magic/woodland-rapport-v0.md), because initiative has **no globally authored physical seconds per round**. It can resolve a hazard early; it does **not** permit time-reset on mode switches, prolong a toxin, or add any extra regeneration. **TX-17** flags potential conservative-shortening balance during repeated transitions.

## 4. Nonstacking, re-exposure and ending

**One active mundane toxin instance per creature in v0**, including an explicitly described post-instant **residual marker**. A newly delivered instance with an **equal or lower printed grade** is ignored while an instance is active: no new resistance roll, damage, extension, reset, additive penalty or fresh onset. A new **higher printed grade** may replace the existing instance **only after valid delivery** and resolves its own one resistance roll; prior active future ticks/penalties stop at replacement; **already-lost HP, Vigor, accrued wounds and completed time effects are never refunded**. If the higher-grade exposure is magically prevented before effect application, do not replace the old active instance. **Use printed grade for precedence even if a successful natural resistance reduces its consequences.** A failed exposure route (e.g. fully stopped attack) cannot replace anything. A tie preserves the earlier instance. Once the instance expires, a genuinely separate later exposure may establish a fresh instance; repeatedly touching the same contaminated surface does not manufacture attacks/checks.

**Continuous penalties** end at their recorded time/tick expiry. **Instant packets** apply only once and leave a **three-turn short residual marker** solely for the nonstack gate; this marker has **no further damage/condition** and expires through the same short-tick/exploration time bookkeeping. **Next-recovery** profiles end when their next specifically scheduled recovery is processed (or their six-hour upper window expires); other hourly instances expire after their stated checkpoints. No effect replaces existing wounds/Repair Progress. A cured or separately neutralized instance ends only if an **actual authored treatment or spell** says it ends it. Merely using ordinary Medicine, rolling again, natural HP regeneration or casting Basic Heal does **not** erase it.

**TX-01 repeated-exposure watch:** Assess whether equal/lower-grade re-exposure should eventually refresh duration, accumulate physical dose, bypass temporary markers or escalate a grade. These are **not** options available under v0.

**TX-02 toxin-only exploration clock / phase watch:** Every completed short interval outside initiative advances **only the toxin's own scheduled harm/penalty/delay/expiry**, and **never** grants natural regeneration, Repair Progress or default per-round Dying loss. Retain sub-six-second phases across repeated transitions; real initiative turns still use their genuine Step 2. **Monitor mode-dependent recovery** until a separately authorized universal exploration regeneration system exists. Five 5-second windows → four hazard intervals and one second retained; 5 seconds → one real turn → one second → two intervals, only **one** real Regeneration Step.

**TX-03 one-instance watch:** Evaluate whether mixed different-family exposures should later coexist with family-scoped nonstacking. One-profile-at-a-time is a deliberate **MVP simplification**, not a claim that real toxins cannot coexist.

### Exact mechanical examples

1. **Natural resistance reduces, does not negate:** An eligible recipient exposed to **TX-HM** rolls **d20+Vitality = 12** against **DC12**. Tie meets DC: schedule **3 future 1-HP ticks**, not five and **not zero**. If they spend Vigor later healing those points, the cost follows ordinary HP regeneration. With **TX-CY**, successful DC12 means **2 immediate HP** rather than four, not zero.
2. **Defenses are separate:** A needle misses or is stopped by Guard/Armor without establishing a genuine breach: **no TX-NE exposure or resistance roll**. If it actually breaches tissue, TX-NE resistance resolves once. −2 applies to **Dodge Defense**, *not* general Agility or already recorded Dodge Pressure.
3. **Toxin clock and regeneration:** A healthy character receives TX-HM during round R, then has its next Regeneration Step (if damage exists) **before** that turn's first scheduled poison tick. A tick can leave **1 newly lost HP** through the opponent's next attack sequence; next Regeneration Step may heal that loss using **1 Vigor** if throughput and resources permit. End after exactly three or five scheduled ticks.
4. **Different nonstacked toxin:** A Minor TX-MY is active with two intervals remaining. A valid Moderate TX-HM can **replace** its future penalty after its new DC12 resistance. A later Minor TX-DE exposure while the Moderate is active is ignored. Already-lost HP/Vigor from earlier effects remains.
5. **Exploration, short hazard:** Failed TX-HM leaves **three 1-HP ticks**, phase 0, and an otherwise healthy **HP 10 / Vigor 10** character. Three completed six-second exploration intervals resolve **three separate 1-HP toxin packets**, but **zero virtual Regeneration/Dying Steps**: ending **HP 7, Vigor 10** absent an independent heal. If already at HP 8 with a TX-CY *residual nonstack marker* for three intervals, after 18 seconds the marker expires with HP still **8**, no added Repair Progress or Vigor expenditure. Entering real initiative before expiration permits only genuinely due **real** Step 2 regeneration, as canonical.
6. **Exploration, long hazard:** Failed TX-NP starts four **hourly** Vigor-loss pulses. At 60/120/180/240 elapsed minutes after exposure, lose 1 Vigor if still active. **No new natural resistance** and no automatic 6-hour recovery at hourly boundaries.
7. **Next Vigor Recovery:** If TX-HE is active before a legitimate scheduled six-hour recovery and normally that event would restore **3 Vigor**, full TX-HE reduces that **single recovery to 1 Vigor**, reduced TX-HE to **2 Vigor**. It never spends/erases Vigor already present. No 6-hour event within the active window means no recovery penalty.
8. **Ongoing toxin versus healing:** Basic Heal may restore real missing HP/Vigor but does **not** stop TX-HM's future HP ticks, recover elapsed impaired-defense intervals or suppress active TX-CA throughput penalties. Vital Shelter protects only **literal incoming direct Vigor-damage packets** (such as TX-NP); it does **not** prevent TX-HM HP ticks, TX-HE decreased Recovery, TX-CA throughput reduction, or TX-NE Dodge loss.
9. **Already Dying:** A toxin tick landing after the Dying character's Step 2 still deals its own HP injury at Step 3 if due. The default Dying −1 HP occurs in Step 2 under its own rule; a poison tick **does not** create another default Dying tick or another 0-HP-crossing wound after target was already at/below zero.
10. **No magical implication:** A mundane necrotizing toxin is **not Death/Plague magic**, is **not an Intrusion cast** requiring Direct Veil resistance, and does **not** make an unopposed adverse spell bypass relevant magical defenses.

## 6. Venom Endurance, Death/Plague and Alchemy boundaries

**Life / Preservation — Venom Endurance (future):** This foundation deliberately provides targets for future bounded protection. An independently authored Veil protective technique may **fully negate** one eligible ordinary toxin application, individual tick or named consequence **if its real profile says so**, with its own limits and actual timing. There is **no automatic +2 toxin save, negation on any Veil-related roll, retroactive repair, toxin purge, generic poison immunity, or free per-tick block**. A future protection might use finite applications/ticks; this page does not authorize any yet.

**Death / Plague (deferred):** Magical imposition/transformation of disease, sickness, decay and infection is separately authored **Death / Plague** content, with the [canonical Death Aspect](../../world/aspect-identities-and-boundaries.md#death) and protected-living-self Intrusion rules where applicable. A mundane necrotizing effect is not inherently Death magic. Conversely a later Plague technique cannot claim an ordinary toxin's successful natural resistance roll as a shortcut around magical pressure/resistance. Neither magical toxin families nor spell tiers, VP, damage, magical resistance, contagion or antidotes are authored here.

**Alchemy (deferred integration only):** Canonical [Concoctions, Elixirs and Transmutation](../../world/aspect-identities-and-boundaries.md#alchemy) already distinguish **real ingredient properties** from **independently authorized Veil expression**. In a future recipe, a source material may leave one of these mundane toxin profiles as an explicit **ingredient-derived side effect** alongside an authored magical benefit; neither grants free poison crafting nor implies every preparation from a toxic ingredient remains toxic. A recipe will need to specify **which material side effects survive processing, their exposure route, grade, persistence, and whether magical mitigation applies**. Do not stack this catalogue with unbuilt magical effects automatically. Medicine, Herbalism and Alchemy remain learned disciplines for appropriate diagnosis/knowledge tasks, **not automatic exposure bypass or universal treatment**.

**World sources (future ecology pass):** Each profile names a **non-specific plant/fungus/fauna/environmental contamination category**, **not a canonical species or actionable manufacturing route**. When world/bestiary authoring begins, record source organism/material, habitat, ecology, actual exposure behavior, dominant family and grade, broad storage/trade restrictions, lawful medical uses, and recipe side-effect eligibility. Extraction, concentration, refining, weapon preparation, economy, contamination spread, disease cycles and real-world chemical instructions remain outside this v0.
