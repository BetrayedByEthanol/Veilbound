# Scholar Foundation

> Canonical extraction from Core Rules Audit v34. Structural level 1–5 rules are promoted here. Repertoire counts and technique-specific numeric values remain provisional where the audit says they are not calibrated.

## Archetype identity

A Scholar accesses the Veil through **learned technical methods** rather than prayer, emotion-linked instinct, or a permanent Anointed bond.

A Scholar does **not** require a Pillar's approval, recognition, favor, or personal relationship. Pillar Aspects are real stable patterns within the Veil; Scholar methods reproduce, enter, route, or shape Volition according to those patterns. The Pillar remains metaphysically relevant because the pattern exists, not because it consciously grants permission.

- A Scholar may potentially learn techniques from **any Pillar or Aspect**.
- Every technique is learned individually.
- Techniques retain their real Pillar/Aspect identity; Scholars do not learn abstract setting-free buffs.
- Ordinary learned techniques resolve at their normal listed effectiveness, VP cost, action requirement, and Veil Control scaling.
- Scholars do not suffer a generalist penalty.
- Scholars receive no automatic Priest Pillar Accord amplification, Gifted emotional amplification, or Anointed Aspect Authority.
- Scholar strength is **configurable breadth**: selection, combination, preparation, technical shaping, and deliberate cross-Pillar repertoire.

The final catalogue/repertoire calibration must preserve Scholar as the archetype with the strongest **player-configurable problem coverage over time**, even though Gifted may possess a large fixed catalogue.

## High Scholar — Projection through synthesis

**Core identity:** broad technical caster and situational synthesist.

**Core question:** *Which known technique solves this, and is combining two techniques worth the extra VP?*

### Levels 1–2 — Technical Repertoire and Prepared Composite Formulae

High Scholar begins with a comparatively broad learned repertoire. Exact techniques-known counts remain provisional.

A High Scholar may use **Composite Casting** with one known Primary Technique and one compatible known Secondary Technique.

At levels 1–2, the exact Primary + Secondary pairing must be prepared as a **composite formula**.

#### Primary Technique

The Primary defines:

- target;
- range;
- area or shape;
- attack/resistance resolution;
- fundamental delivery chassis.

The Primary resolves at full normal strength.

#### Secondary Technique

The Secondary is incorporated into the Primary rather than resolving as a second independent technique.

By default it contributes:

- approximately half of normal scalable numerical output; and
- a diminished version of one compatible defining special property, where applicable.

It does not normally retain independent targeting, a separate attack roll, its full original area, its full original duration, or every original rider.

#### Cost and action

```
Composite VP Cost =
full Primary VP cost + full Secondary VP cost
```

There is no default discount. Composite Casting provides **action compression**, not VP efficiency.

Use the slower action requirement of the two components:

- Projection Action + Projection Action → **1 Projection Action**;
- Projection Action + Full Action → **1 Full Action**;
- Full Action + Full Action → **1 Full Action**.

Ordinary single-technique casting remains fully competent and is often the more resource-efficient choice.

### Level 2 — Expanded Repertoire

Increase the learned toolbox. Exact counts remain provisional.

This is breadth, not a generic damage/healing/potency bonus.

### Level 3 — Formula Fluency

Prepared-pairing restriction ends.

Any compatible pair of known techniques may be synthesized at casting time without preparing that exact pair in advance.

Formula Fluency does not reduce VP cost or remove the normal Secondary reduction.

### Level 4 — Expanded Repertoire + Ability Increase

Gain:

- the universal level-4 **+1 Ability** increase; and
- another meaningful repertoire expansion.

No generic Scholar-specific potency multiplier is added.

### Level 5 — Advanced Synthesis

When Composite Casting, choose one Secondary emphasis.

**Numerical Emphasis**

- Secondary contributes approximately half normal numerical output.
- Incidental special properties are strongly diminished.

**Property Emphasis**

- Secondary contributes little or no numerical output.
- One compatible defining non-numerical property is preserved substantially more strongly.

Examples include knockback, regeneration suppression, concealment, restraint, interruption, or terrain alteration.

The purpose is to construct the **right answer**, not simply stack raw output.

### High Scholar boundary

Composite Casting is a situational technical advantage, not a mandatory rotation.

The High Scholar must support combat caster, battlemage, controller, support/healer, generalist, and deliberately focused repertoires.

## Medium Scholar — Routing and repertoire

**Core identity:** rapid allocation of committed inward Veil power plus individually learned cross-Pillar techniques.

**Core question:** *Where should my power be routed right now?*

### Inward Boosts and channels

Certain learned techniques have the **Inward** property.

An Inward Boost:

- commits its listed VP;
- normally has no ongoing action cost once active;
- remains active until released or rerouted;
- occupies an applicable **Inward Channel**.

Representative channels include:

- Weapon;
- Motor Control;
- Reflex;
- Locomotion;
- Body;
- Senses;
- Regeneration.

Two limits apply simultaneously:

1. the Medium Scholar has a maximum number of active Inward Boosts;
2. only one active Boost may occupy the same channel.

### Rerouting

Rerouting replaces one active compatible Inward Boost with another known compatible Inward Boost while redistributing committed power.

- no action cost;
- no per-round frequency limit;
- levels 1–2: **3 Spent VP** per reroute;
- levels 3–5: **2 Spent VP** per reroute.

Rerouting does not itself create an outward technique or waive that technique's normal activation action.

The universal zero-action/no-refresh rule applies: rerouting does not grant interrupt timing and does not refresh limited output.

### Overrouting

Overrouting pushes an Inward Boost beyond ordinary output by drawing capability away from a defined competing function.

- benefit and sacrifice are authored by the technique;
- normal maximum: **2 steps**;
- **+1 Spent VP per Overroute step** in addition to the reroute cost;
- dropping back to the normal version of the same Boost costs no action and no VP;
- pushing into Overroute again requires paying the cost again.

### Medium Scholar progression

| Level | Foundation feature |
|---:|---|
| 1 | **Veil Routing** — max 1 active Inward Boost; reroute costs 3 Spent VP |
| 2 | **Technical Repertoire** — broaden learned technical options; counts provisional |
| 3 | **Efficient Routing + Overrouting** — reroute becomes 2 Spent VP; Overrouting enters mature loop |
| 4 | **Dual Routing + Ability Increase** — max 2 active Inward Boosts; +1 Ability |
| 5 | **Matrix Reconfiguration** — one rerouting payment may replace both active Boosts at once; Overroute surcharges still apply |

### Medium Scholar boundary

Medium Scholar changes **what function** inward Veil power performs.

It must remain distinct from:

- Medium Priest, which changes **who receives** a Consecration;
- Medium Gifted, whose established conduits **retune expression** with emotion;
- Medium Anointed, which changes the **Vessel** receiving one permanent Aspect.

## Low Scholar — Prepared Embodiment

**Core identity:** prepared, martial-first, cross-Pillar technical toolbox.

**Core question:** *What did I prepare, what is worth activating, and when is spending scarce VP better than simply fighting?*

The Low Scholar remains fully capable of ordinary combat without magic.

### Known, Prepared, and Active

Low Scholar techniques exist in three layers:

1. **Known Techniques** — all learned techniques.
2. **Prepared Techniques** — a limited ready subset; exact counts remain provisional.
3. **Active Embodiments** — the much smaller set of prepared inward/equipment techniques currently sustained.

### Embodiments and switching

An Embodiment is a prepared inward or equipment-focused technique affecting the Scholar or immediately used equipment.

The Low Scholar does **not** gain Medium-style rerouting.

To switch:

1. release the existing effect;
2. activate the replacement normally.

Released Committed VP returns to Available VP, but previously Spent VP remains Spent.

This makes changing configuration possible but intentionally inefficient.

### Active Embodiment limit

- levels 1–3: maximum **1 Active Embodiment**;
- levels 4–5: maximum **2 Active Embodiments**.

Channel conflicts still apply.

Low Focus does not prohibit outward techniques. Prepared outward effects use their normal action and VP costs, but the smaller Low-Focus VP pool makes them selective expenditures.

### Low Scholar progression

| Level | Foundation feature |
|---:|---|
| 1 | **Prepared Arsenal** — limited prepared loadout; max 1 Active Embodiment |
| 2 | **Field Preparation** — broaden learned repertoire and prepared loadout; counts provisional |
| 3 | **Deep Embodiment** — compatible Active Embodiments may be **Intensified** by paying technique-defined additional VP |
| 4 | **Dual Embodiment + Ability Increase** — max 2 Active Embodiments; +1 Ability |
| 5 | **Entrenched Configuration** — if an Embodiment has remained unchanged since the start of the previous turn, its next Intensification costs 1 VP less, minimum 1 unless explicitly allowed to reach 0 |

Changing or replacing an Embodiment breaks its Entrenched state.

### Low Scholar boundary

Low Scholar rewards **preparation and commitment**.

Its preferred escalation is to Intensify a good prepared choice rather than repeatedly replace it.

## Three-Focus Scholar distinction

| Focus | Scholar expression |
|---|---|
| High | **Synthesize** known techniques into situational composite answers |
| Medium | **Reroute** committed power among learned inward functions |
| Low | **Prepare and commit** to a small active Embodiment configuration |
