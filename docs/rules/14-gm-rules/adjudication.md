# GM Adjudication

> Canonical extraction from Core Rules Audit v34. This chapter covers the GM procedures already locked by the native resolution engine and identifies the GM-facing systems that do not yet exist.

## Ask first: does this need a roll?

Do **not** call for a roll simply because an action could theoretically fail.

Routine or trivial competence is automatic when the character can reasonably perform the action and failure would not create meaningful uncertainty.

Training can make an action routine for one character that remains uncertain for another.

## Choose the resolution mode

### Passive

Use:

```
10 + Ability + applicable Training + situational modifiers
```

Use passive resolution for things the character naturally does or notices without deliberately spending time on the task.

### Safe active effort

When repeated attempts are possible and failure itself has no serious consequence, allow the player to:

- roll;
- Take 10;
- Take 20.

The GM determines the fictional time consumed.

Take 20 means exhaustive effort. It does not override impossibility and is not literal automatic success.

### Pressure or meaningful consequence

When time is limited, pressure matters, or failure has meaningful consequences, roll:

```
d20 + Ability + applicable Training + situational modifiers
```

Take 10 and Take 20 are unavailable.

## Set difficulty from circumstances

General target:

```
10 + Difficulty Modifier
```

| Difficulty | Modifier | Target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Easy under pressure | -3 | 7 |
| Favorable | -1 | 9 |
| Standard | +0 | 10 |
| Challenging | +2 | 12 |
| Hard | +5 | 15 |
| Exceptional | +8 | 18 |
| Extreme | +10 | 20 |

Tasks easier than this ladder are normally automatic.

Higher targets are possible for genuinely extraordinary situations.

Difficulty belongs to the **actual circumstances**, not permanently to an object.

A wall is not always “DC 15.” Weather, tools, surface condition, route, time, assistance, injury, visibility, and chosen approach can change whether a roll is needed and what its difficulty is.

## Use the actual Ability

Do not force every recognizable activity into one fixed Ability.

Ask what the character is doing.

Examples:

- climbing by raw upper-body force → Strength;
- climbing through balance/technical movement → Agility;
- diagnosing illness → Intellect;
- surgery → Precision;
- noticing movement → Awareness;
- persuading or commanding → Presence.

Relevant Training may pair with different Abilities in different circumstances.

## Approach changes circumstances, not character stats

Players decide **what they try**.

Character rules determine what the character knows and how effectively they execute it.

A strong plan may:

- reduce difficulty;
- remove the need for a roll;
- make an otherwise impossible outcome plausible;
- change the consequence of failure.

A poor plan may do the opposite.

Do not require the player to personally possess the character's social eloquence, technical education, medical knowledge, or other expertise.

Likewise, do not erase player deduction simply because the character has low Intellect when the player is reasoning from clues already supplied.

## Give automatic knowledge when appropriate

If a character with relevant Training would simply know a fact, provide it.

Do not turn every piece of expertise into a d20 check.

Roll when:

- the knowledge is obscure;
- memory is uncertain under pressure;
- evidence is incomplete;
- conflicting interpretations matter;
- the answer is genuinely uncertain and consequential.

## Natural 1 / Natural 20 boundary

The current audit is internally inconsistent on non-combat natural-1/natural-20 handling: the detailed Core Resolution section leaves it subject to final review, while a later audit summary says ability-check 1/20 behavior was resolved as worst/best plausible outcome.

Until that contradiction is deliberately resolved, the canonical non-combat engine does **not require** a special natural-1/natural-20 result.

For attacks, use the separate combat rules.

## Procedures still missing

The following GM-facing systems are not yet defined:

- helping/group checks;
- a universal opposed-check rule;
- extended tasks/progress clocks;
- regional Pillar-state tracking;
- NPC construction;
- creature/bestiary construction;
- encounter difficulty/budgeting;
- treasure/reward generation;
- campaign/faction/settlement clocks;
- extended social/investigation conflicts;
- travel/exploration procedure.

Do not silently replace these with inherited 5e mechanics.
